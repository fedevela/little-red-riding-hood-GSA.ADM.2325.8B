# Dramatic Arcs — Pressures and Possibilities

## Scope of These Arcs

These foundations describe sustained desires, changing pressures, and possible transformations. They do not allocate scenes, order beats, supply dialogue, or determine performance choices. The Wolf makes several attempts before its final trap; the exact number, order, and enactment remain unwritten.

## The Wolf’s Gradual Trap

The Wolf’s appetite is immediate; its approach is patient. It does not begin with the final trap, and its earlier approaches cannot all succeed cleanly. Red’s questions, refusals, and partial cooperation require it to revise what it offers.

Its repertoire includes apparently harmless interest that gathers information, helpfulness that seeks dependence, flattery aimed at Red’s wish for independence, and manufactured urgency that presents compliance as protection. These are connected strategies, not a mandatory four-step sequence. An unsuccessful approach can still reveal something; an apparent success can also make Red more suspicious.

The final trap concentrates the underlying contradiction: safety offered under the predator’s control. Earlier interactions must make that offer intelligible without making Red’s acceptance inevitable. Its concrete mechanism belongs to future dramatic development.

## Red’s Arc — little_red_riding_hood

Red begins with a purpose of her own: carrying provisions to grandmother and proving to herself that she can be trusted with the journey. She is observant, but has more practice recognizing open disapproval than concealed designs.

Her internal pressure lies between protecting her judgment and wanting that judgment to be recognized by someone else. She can question help, refuse a proposal, reconsider, or conceal her doubts. Her responses affect the Wolf’s approach; she is not simply carried through its plan.

Her possible transformation is toward claiming her own perception even without permission or certainty. The bible does not guarantee that this inward movement provides an outward escape.

## The Wolf’s Arc — wolf

The Wolf begins with hunger and confidence in its ability to make attention feel like kindness. Its conflict is tactical and personal: it wants control while preserving the story that Red is freely choosing what it proposes.

Resistance tests its patience. It can soften, redirect, or appeal to another desire, but each adjustment risks exposing the appetite beneath its concern. Its increasing investment in the trap makes refusal harder for it to tolerate.

The final offer concentrates that need for control. There is no established redemption, triumph, or defeat.

## Relationship Evolution

An unequal acquaintance may acquire the appearance of companionship without becoming mutual trust. The Wolf seeks information and influence; Red weighs usefulness against unease. Every exchange can change how much each believes the other understands. Neither character has direct access to the other’s private thoughts.

## Climax and Resolution Boundary

The final question remains unanswered. Red faces a credible danger and a proposed refuge whose terms are controlled by the Wolf. The final door is neither open nor closed: entry, retreat, capture, and survival are not settled. This is the boundary of the ending, not a literal door movement or a prescribed final image.

## Relationship to Future Outlines

No season, episode, scene, or beat is assigned here. Future outlines may draw on these pressures while preserving several attempts, consequential resistance, and the unresolved ending. Existing Script materials remain untouched.
