# Wolf — Secrets

## Central Secret

Its attention serves a predatory intention. The offered relationship is a means of obtaining control over Red.

## Lesser Secrets

Its approaches are uncertain experiments, not the expression of a flawless plan. It needs information and can be wrong. The safety it ultimately offers would place its own conditions over Red’s freedom.

The apparent favor of its attention has a price: it wants Red’s company and closeness to follow from what it offers. It would prefer her gratitude to feel like a debt. The possibility that she might tell someone about the encounter threatens the kindly account it gives of itself.

## Who Knows and Who Does Not

The Wolf knows its intention and the claims it knowingly invents. Red begins without that private knowledge and may develop suspicion through eligible observations; the timing and extent of discovery remain unwritten.

## Reasons for Concealment

If Red could fully assess its purpose, the appearance of disinterested help would lose force.

## Consequences of Disclosure

Exposure threatens its influence but does not, by itself, establish Red’s escape or the Wolf’s defeat.

## Truths Hidden from Self

It calls resistance misunderstanding so that it need not acknowledge a will separate from its own. Its supposed generosity is conditional upon getting what it wants.

It wants its personal appetite to look like a fair reason for granting or withholding help. Being specially attentive can make the bargain appear affectionate even before anything has been agreed. Its wish for closeness establishes neither Red’s welcome nor a bond between them.
