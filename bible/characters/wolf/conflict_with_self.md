# Wolf — Conflict with Self

## The divided self

Patient, observant, adaptable, and possessive. It can listen closely without caring for the person it hears.

It remembers revealed details and can revise an unsuccessful approach. Persistence brings it back after disappointment, still capable of misreading what it sees.

It mistakes access for entitlement and resistance for a difficulty it should be able to solve. Pride makes another person’s continuing independence difficult to bear.

It prizes control while wanting its influence to look voluntary. It treats its own patience as generosity, though the patience serves hunger.

It takes being welcomed as proof that its attention is welcome in every form. When Red’s wish differs, it is tempted to judge her youth, her hood, or her home instead of accepting the difference. Those judgments protect its pride from the difficulty of accepting her separate will.

Open force could expose what persuasion conceals. It wants the advantage of being trusted without accepting the limits that trust would require.

## What fear asks of it

It fears losing influence before it has secured the conditions of the trap. Being recognized clearly, meeting a persistent refusal, or discovering that it has read Red poorly each threatens its confidence in its own patience.

Revisions can expose the underlying design. The more it insists on its helpfulness, the harder it may become to preserve that appearance.
