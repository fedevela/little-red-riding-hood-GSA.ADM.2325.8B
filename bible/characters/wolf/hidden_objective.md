# Wolf — Hidden Objective

## The appetite beneath the appetite

Beyond reaching its prey, it wants to feel that another will can be made to orbit its own. It would possess both the outcome and the appearance of having been welcomed. Hunger is plain to it; the wish to make its hunger seem deserved can hide inside the name it gives its patience.

Red’s separate will troubles the possession it imagines. It would like her freedom to become another difficulty its cleverness can solve.

## What remains concealed

Its attention serves a predatory intention. The offered relationship is a means of obtaining control over Red.

Its approaches are uncertain experiments. It needs information and can misread what it finds. The safety it ultimately offers would place its own conditions over Red’s freedom.

The apparent favor of its attention has a price: it wants Red’s company and closeness to follow from what it offers. It would prefer her gratitude to feel like a debt. The possibility that she might tell someone about the encounter threatens the kindly account it gives of itself.

If Red could fully assess its purpose, the appearance of disinterested help would lose force.

## What the character cannot readily admit

It calls resistance misunderstanding so that it need not acknowledge a will separate from its own. Its supposed generosity is conditional upon getting what it wants.

It wants its personal appetite to look like a fair reason for granting or withholding help. Being specially attentive can make the bargain appear affectionate even before anything has been agreed. It imagines a bond in the space where it hopes to find her welcome.
