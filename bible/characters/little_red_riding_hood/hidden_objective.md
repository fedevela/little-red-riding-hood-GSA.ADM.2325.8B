# Red — Hidden Objective

## The private longing

Beneath the errand lives a wish she may find harder to confess: to be trusted before she has proved herself, and to feel that trust taking root inside her. Part of her wants the journey to belong to her as an adventure as well as a duty. Recognition draws her toward another’s attention; the freedom to remain herself matters more deeply than the praise she hopes to receive.

Her own attention might become a place to begin, even while she still longs for another person’s approval.

## The account she hopes to carry home

She wants to arrive with her own account of the journey intact: what she welcomed, what troubled her, and what she chose. Being able to tell someone matters even while she is still finding words for herself.

## What remains concealed

She is less certain of her competence than she wants a stranger to see. She keeps that uncertainty close.

She may keep a suspicion private while deciding what it means.

She wants to preserve dignity and room to decide. Disclosure to someone untrusted may feel like giving away an advantage.

She may fear that speaking too soon will turn apparent help against her. Keeping a doubt private can preserve room to think while unease gathers beneath her courtesy. She still wants a listener to whom she could carry the truth safely.

## What the character cannot readily admit

She can confuse needing time or help with being incapable.
