# Red — Fears

## Greatest Fear

That she will recognize the danger only after the freedom to refuse has narrowed.

## Secondary Fears

Failing someone she loves; being judged incapable; discovering that asking for help has placed her under another’s terms.

She fears that refusing the Wolf will change the kindness it has offered, or that speaking about her unease will make the path harder. These are possibilities she worries about, not knowledge of what the Wolf will do.

## Triggers

An explanation that changes under questioning, concern that dismisses her judgment, or an offer that makes refusal sound like wrongdoing.

## Avoidance Tactics

She can try to settle uncertainty privately or make a refusal gentler than she feels. These are tendencies, not required actions.

## Cost of Avoidance

Protecting the appearance of confidence can delay naming a problem. The Wolf remains responsible for exploiting that delay.

The effort of weighing its moods can crowd out her own purpose. A journey made for love begins to demand attention she never meant to give the Wolf.
