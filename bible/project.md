# Little Red Riding Hood — Project

## Official Title

Little Red Riding Hood

## Conceptual Subtitle

The Door Between — a working subtitle for a story whose answer remains on the threshold.

## Dramatic Logline

A girl carrying provisions through the woods meets a Wolf whose kindness changes shape whenever she questions it. As the promise of help becomes a narrowing choice, she must reckon with a danger she can name before she knows how to leave.

## Premise and Conflict Engine

Red is travelling to her grandmother’s home. The Wolf wants to make her available to its hunger, and prefers to make its designs resemble her own decisions. It tries several approaches, learning from refusals and partial concessions before attempting its final trap. Red’s obligations, curiosity, and wish to be capable give the encounter its tension; they do not make her responsible for the Wolf’s intentions.

The final trap rests on an offer of safety whose conditions the Wolf controls. Whether Red enters those conditions remains unanswered. The bible establishes this conflict, not its performed sequence.

## Primary and Secondary Genres

Folk-tale drama and psychological suspense, with the intimate scale of a two-character theatrical encounter.

## Audience

Teens. The story speaks to the wish for independence, the weight of another person’s approval, and the uneasy discovery that attention can conceal an appetite for control. Red’s doubt deserves to be taken seriously even before she can explain it.

## Season 1

Red’s journey and her encounter with the Wolf form the season’s central movement. Several attempts draw their competing desires into sharper conflict, gathering toward the final trap and the unanswered threshold.

## Narrative Hooks

When does help become a claim upon another person? What does a refusal cost when the person offering relief also defines the threat? How much must Red know before her doubt deserves to be trusted?

the ideas in the lyrical language of this world. Keep bureaucratic definitions,
institutional vocabulary, and rigid compliance phrasing out of the handoff and

Conceptual coverage is a creative responsibility, not a requirement to stage one

### Receiving the Ideas Through the Characters

The Wolf listens, revises, and tries again. A condition hidden in help, a change
of warmth after refusal, and the burden of managing its attention can grow from
the same relationship. They need not become separate incidents, one per source
section. Let the ideas overlap naturally while keeping their different pressures
intelligible.

Red has a purpose, curiosity, and judgment before any of these pressures arise.
She can welcome one kindness and decline another form of attention. Her response
can change. Courtesy, silence, or an earlier concession does not surrender her
freedom to choose distance now. Responsibility for the Wolf's designs remains
with the Wolf.

The source's sexual dimensions are received through unwanted personal closeness,
entitlement, and conditional favor. This adaptation carries those ideas through
the existing folk-tale encounter for teens. It establishes no sexual events or
mutual intimate relationship. The writer has room to convey the pressure without
changing that foundation.

For 1.4, the two-character cast limits the analogy: the story does not establish
other recipients of favors or observers comparing treatment. Apparent favor can
be legible in the relationship Red experiences. Preserve that conceptual reach
without claiming the story represents every circumstance in the source.

### What Remains Open

The writer discovers the specific attempts, dialogue, and actions. Several
attempts precede the final trap; Red's responses affect the Wolf's approach.
The final door is neither open nor closed. Tragedy remains possible, and the
question of how Red can act remains available for discussion.

The unresolved fate does not make the story undecided about Red's dignity.
Neither a stated lesson nor a successful rescue is required to make her claim
to freedom intelligible.

and the character foundations. During authorial review, consider whether each

Where an idea remains only an intention, identify that gap honestly and develop
it through the characters. Assess conceptual coverage rather than borrowed

During roundtable preparation, preserve character knowledge boundaries. This
shared authorial explanation does not tell Red the Wolf's private purpose or
supply either character with the other's thoughts. The director and preparation
process translate relevant pressures into each character's eligible context.

## Authoritative Sources

The traditional Little Red Riding Hood tale supplies the shared starting motifs: Red, the Wolf, a woodland journey, and a visit to grandmother. No edition, translation, or adaptation has been selected as textual authority, and no source dialogue is reproduced.

The partner’s directions establish this adaptation: two performing characters; other people permitted offstage; several slow attempts before the Wolf’s final trap; an unresolved, possibly tragic ending; a final door neither open nor closed; discussion without a supplied answer. All additional characterization here is original to this project and remains revisable by the partner.

The partner also supplied a thematic source concerning unwanted attention, conditional favors, dignity, and the freedom to speak. Its ideas enter this adaptation through the characters’ relationship and the changing experience of the path. See the [source record](../README.md#thematic-source) for attribution.
