# Little Red Riding Hood — Project

## Official Title

Little Red Riding Hood

## Conceptual Subtitle

The Door Between — a working subtitle for a story whose answer remains on the threshold.

## Dramatic Logline

A girl carrying provisions through the woods meets a Wolf whose kindness changes shape whenever she questions it. As the promise of help becomes a narrowing choice, she must reckon with a danger she can name before she knows how to leave.

## Premise and Conflict Engine

Red is travelling to her grandmother’s home. The Wolf wants to make her available to its hunger, and prefers to make its designs resemble her own decisions. It tries several approaches, learning from refusals and partial concessions before attempting its final trap. Red’s obligations, curiosity, and wish to be capable give the encounter its tension. The Wolf bears responsibility for the designs it brings to that meeting.

The final trap rests on an offer of safety whose conditions the Wolf controls. Whether Red enters those conditions remains unanswered.

## Primary and Secondary Genres

Folk-tale drama and psychological suspense, with the intimate scale of a two-character theatrical encounter.

## Audience

Teens. The story speaks to the wish for independence, the weight of another person’s approval, and the uneasy discovery that attention can conceal an appetite for control. Red’s doubt deserves to be taken seriously even before she can explain it.

## Season 1

Red’s journey and her encounter with the Wolf form the season’s central movement. Several attempts draw their competing desires into sharper conflict, gathering toward the final trap and the unanswered threshold.

## Narrative Hooks

When does help become a claim upon another person? What does a refusal cost when the person offering relief also defines the threat? How much must Red know before her doubt deserves to be trusted?

## GSA Concepts — Dramaturgy First

Human desires, unequal power, and the cost of choices carry the source ideas
into this story. Character, subtext, tension, and consequence give them a place
in the lyrical language of the woods.

### Receiving the Ideas Through the Characters

The Wolf listens, revises, and tries again. A condition hidden in help, a change
of warmth after refusal, and the burden of managing its attention can grow from
the same relationship. Their pressures overlap while each carries its own cost.

Red has a purpose, curiosity, and judgment before these pressures arise. She can
welcome one kindness and decline another form of attention. Her freedom to
choose distance continues through courtesy, silence, and earlier concessions.
Responsibility for the Wolf’s designs remains with the Wolf.

The source’s sexual dimensions enter this teen folk tale as unwanted personal
closeness, entitlement, and conditional favor. The reception of 1.4 is partial:
its focus is the displacement of Red’s purpose and the apparent favor offered
within this pair. Comparisons among other recipients or observers fall beyond
that analogy. The two-character encounter gives these selected ideas their form.

### The Unanswered Door

Several attempts precede the final trap; Red’s responses change the Wolf’s
approach. The final door is neither open nor closed. Tragedy remains possible,
and the question of how Red can act remains alive beyond the encounter.
Her fate is uncertain; her claim to dignity is clear.

## Authoritative Sources

The traditional Little Red Riding Hood tale supplies the shared starting motifs: Red, the Wolf, a woodland journey, and a visit to grandmother. The partner’s directions shape this original adaptation.

The partner’s directions establish this adaptation: two performing characters; other people permitted offstage; several slow attempts before the Wolf’s final trap; an unresolved, possibly tragic ending; a final door neither open nor closed; discussion without a supplied answer. All additional characterization here is original to this project and remains revisable by the partner.
