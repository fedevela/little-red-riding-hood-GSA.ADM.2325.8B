# Little Red Riding Hood — Project

## Official Title

Little Red Riding Hood

## Conceptual Subtitle

The Door Between — a working subtitle for a story whose answer remains on the threshold.

## Dramatic Logline

A girl carrying provisions through the woods meets a Wolf whose kindness changes shape whenever she questions it. As the promise of help becomes a narrowing choice, she must reckon with a danger she can name before she knows how to leave.

## Premise and Conflict Engine

Red is travelling to her grandmother’s home. The Wolf wants to make her available to its hunger, and prefers to make its designs resemble her own decisions. It tries several approaches, learning from refusals and partial concessions before attempting its final trap. Red’s obligations, curiosity, and wish to be capable give the encounter its tension; they do not make her responsible for the Wolf’s intentions.

The final trap rests on an offer of safety whose conditions the Wolf controls. Whether Red enters those conditions remains unanswered. The bible establishes this conflict, not its performed sequence.

## Primary and Secondary Genres

Folk-tale drama and psychological suspense, with the intimate scale of a two-character theatrical encounter.

## Target Audience

An audience invited to discuss trust, coercion, responsibility, and the space between recognizing danger and finding a way out.

## Audience Demographics

No fixed age band is established. The material carries threatened predation and the possibility of tragedy; audience age and presentation context remain production decisions.

## Audience Interests and Expectations

A familiar red hood and an unfamiliar uncertainty. The audience may recognize the danger before Red does, yet should still understand why an offer might matter to her.

## Theatrical Context and Points of Difference

A forum-theatre adaptation with an unresolved central question. Its distinction is the Wolf’s patient, revisable approach and Red’s developing discernment, rather than a settled moral or guaranteed rescue. No particular production is a canonical reference.

## Format and Technical Specifications

A live theatrical work with exactly two performing characters: Little Red Riding Hood and the Wolf. Offstage family may be mentioned but receive no performed roles or profiles. Technical design remains undecided.

## Target Performance Duration

Undecided; no duration is imposed by this bible.

## Production Unit

One theatrical work. Season and episode counts are not applicable to its canon; the existing project hierarchy does not establish a serialized format.

## Target Presentation Context

Live forum theatre. Venue, distribution, and any recording format remain undecided.

## Key Distinguishing Qualities

A trap that grows slowly; a girl whose resistance matters; a predator who must listen in order to manipulate; a conclusion that withholds certainty without withholding danger.

## Narrative Hooks

When does help become a claim upon another person? What does a refusal cost when the person offering relief also defines the threat? How much must Red know before her doubt deserves to be trusted?

## Authoritative Sources

The traditional Little Red Riding Hood tale supplies the shared starting motifs: Red, the Wolf, a woodland journey, and a visit to grandmother. No edition, translation, or adaptation has been selected as textual authority, and no source dialogue is reproduced.

The partner’s directions establish this adaptation: two performing characters; other people permitted offstage; several slow attempts before the Wolf’s final trap; an unresolved, possibly tragic ending; a final door neither open nor closed; discussion without a supplied answer. All additional characterization here is original to this project and remains revisable by the partner.

## Open Questions and Production Decisions

Duration, audience age, venue, costume realization, and technical design remain undecided. The exact age of Red and a historical date are deliberately unspecified. The ending’s unanswered question is an artistic commitment, not missing content. Dialogue, scenes, beats, blocking, and the concrete realization of the trap belong to later work outside this bible.
