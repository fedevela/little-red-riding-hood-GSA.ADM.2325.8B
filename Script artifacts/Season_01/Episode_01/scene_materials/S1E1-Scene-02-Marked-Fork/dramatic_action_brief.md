# Dramatic Action Brief — S1E1-Scene-02-Marked-Fork

## Scene function

The scene inherits Red on the rain-marked woodland trace in late-day light after
rain. The ordinary road is blocked by a fallen beech; a narrow trace remains
visible beside it. Red carries the covered basket, keeps one hand available, and
the earlier two-twig return mark remains behind her. The Wolf stays at or near
the road edge and must not block either route.

The practical testing sequence is one continuous action: Red withholds the
destination and remaining distance, places a provisional twig mark, compares the
Wolf’s account with dry bark, a wet streak, a split root, a shallow dip, and the
remembered turn, then tests only the ground she can reach. The reliable gain is
limited: one confident claim is shown to be incomplete because the named
landmark cannot support all of its detail. The cost is spent daylight, divided
attention, exposed route method, and a Wolf newly able to shape its guidance
around the evidence Red trusts. No tested patch establishes a safe route.

Character choices remain open. The brief supplies observable pressures,
knowledge boundaries, and playable affordances; it does not prescribe dialogue
or discretionary responses. Mother and grandmother remain offstage. They have no
performed action or independent knowledge in this scene; grandmother functions
as Red’s destination and stake through Red’s established purpose.

## Dramatic questions held in performance

- Can Red keep a useful fact without granting the Wolf her destination?
- What does the Wolf do when a precise question exposes that its certainty outruns
  what the landmark can show?
- Can Red retain the method as her own when the Wolf has noticed it and offers to
  become its interpreter?
- Does the pause produce enough discernment to change her next step, while still
  leaving her without a confirmed safe exit?
