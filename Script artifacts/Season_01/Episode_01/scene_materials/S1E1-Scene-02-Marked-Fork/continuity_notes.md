## Findings

Continuity does not hold across the scene transition. The target scene resets
Red and the Wolf to an earlier location without an established return or
flashback cue. The performed text itself is internally consistent from its
opening fork through its final tested patch, but that local continuity
contradicts the preceding performed progression.

- **Chronology and location contradiction:** The preceding Scene 4 ends with
  Red several steps beyond the flat stones, continuing along the narrowing
  wet trace beneath closing branches, while the Wolf remains in the bracken
  outside the route. Scene 5 then opens at the broad fallen beech blocking
  the ordinary road, with the narrow trace beside it. The earlier continuity
  notes place that beech and fork before the creek, welcome, and interrogation
  scenes. No scene break or stage direction establishes that Red returned to
  the fork, and no flashback or temporal shift is signaled.
- **Props and route markers:** Scene 4 establishes the basket fastening
  tightened on the flat stones and the creek’s angled branch and three short
  far-bank marks left behind. Scene 5 reintroduces the earlier two-twig mark
  and a new twig at the beech, as though the later creek crossing and route
  progress have not occurred. The new provisional mark is physically coherent
  within Scene 5, but its presence at the end of the current preceding
  sequence requires an unperformed return.
- **Physical state and movement:** Scene 4 leaves the basket intact and Red
  moving forward on the trace. Scene 5 begins with her crouching beside the
  fallen beech and ends with her standing at the bracken edge. This is a
  backward relocation, not ordinary offstage travel that can be inferred from
  the established route; it reverses the direction and terrain progression
  recorded in the earlier handoffs.
- **Character knowledge:** Scene 4 has already established that Red knows the
  creek crossing, the narrowing trace, the pale stones, and the Wolf’s
  maintained distance. Scene 5 treats the fork and the Wolf’s account of the
  dip as a first route test, without acknowledging those later observations.
  The Wolf likewise appears at the earlier road edge rather than after its
  established position in the bracken. This is a continuity reset, not a new
  inference or deliberate uncertainty within the characters’ knowledge.
