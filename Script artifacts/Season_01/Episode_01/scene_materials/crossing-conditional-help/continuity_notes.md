## Findings

One transition continuity question requires attention; the scene’s internal
chronology, physical action, props, and character knowledge otherwise hold.

- **Transition / Wolf location — question for the originating character:**
  Scene 1 ends with the Wolf “at the road edge” while Red continues beyond the
  bend into the wet-marked trace (Scene 1 final passage, local beat
  `comment-5577156092-next:e1:b6`, pivotal identity `BEAT-G2-3-1`). Scene 2
  opens with the Wolf at the creek’s near bank and has it remain there through
  the crossing (local beats `070275abd30b:e1:b1`–`b3`), but no passage or
  transition establishes how it moved from the road edge to the creek. The
  accepted preceding continuity note explicitly hands off the Wolf at or near
  the road edge. This is an unaccounted transition, not evidence that the Wolf
  could not have followed the route. The originating character should establish
  the movement or a continuous placement in a future performance if the
  transition must be made explicit.

- **Chronology and setting:** The late-day, post-rain progression is
  consistent. Scene 1 ends as the last light thins; Scene 2 begins with
  daylight thinning and ends with the woods darkening. The creek is a plausible
  continuation of the rain-marked trace, and no time jump is introduced.

- **Red’s location and movement:** Red enters the damaged creek crossing from
  the trace, tests the near bank and stones, crosses to the far bank, and
  continues along the narrowing trace. This preserves the prior scene’s
  direction beyond the bend; the blocked ordinary road and two-twig return mark
  remain behind her and are not contradicted.

- **Props and physical state:** Red retains the covered basket throughout and
  keeps it protected while crossing. The branch introduced at the creek is
  first tested, then angled by the Wolf, and remains above the far-bank mud
  after Red leaves. Red’s three short marks remain visible from the trace. The
  branch and marks do not replace or erase the earlier two-twig mark; they are
  later route traces. No injury, loss of provisions, or unsafe crossing is
  falsely carried forward.

- **Character knowledge:** Red knows only what she can see and test: the water,
  stones, branch, and far bank. Her line “The mark is mine” establishes
  ownership of her independent route record, not knowledge of the Wolf’s later
  plan. The Wolf observes the crossing, branch, mark, and Red’s departure, but
  its intention to make future help seem necessary remains its fallible plan,
  not knowledge Red has acquired. This is consistent with the world’s limits.

- **Transition out:** The scene leaves Red moving onward with the basket,
  destination, and judgment intact. The angled branch and three short marks
  remain at the creek, while the Wolf remains beside the crossing after Red’s
  footsteps fade. A later scene inherits the creek marker, Red’s continued
  progress, thinning light, and the Wolf’s attempted conversion of useful
  guidance into future dependence. Capture, escape, rescue, reconciliation, and
  the final threshold remain unresolved.
