# The Marked Crossing — Dramatic Action Brief

## Scene frame

This is a bounded action brief for Act 1, `crossing-conditional-help`, at a
washed-out creek inside the rain-marked trace, late in the day after rain. The
scene inherits Red's chosen direction beyond the fork, her secured basket, her
free testing hand, the two-twig return mark behind her, and the blocked ordinary
road. The Wolf remains a stranger with terrain knowledge and observable access
to Red's actions, not to her private motives.

The practical favor is a soaked forked branch bent toward a line of stable
stones. It can provide a visible handhold and sequence for this crossing, but it
does not guarantee the far bank or the route beyond it. Red may use the
information and the branch while retaining her basket, pace, destination,
distance, and judgment. The branch's visible trace gives the Wolf a material
opening to imagine that later progress can be made to require its guidance; that
interpretation remains unconfirmed and does not remove Red's ability to proceed.

The creek should remain bodily and testable: shifting water, unstable footing,
wet wood, finite light, and the protected provisions. No action in this brief
decides whether Red trusts the Wolf, draws closer, or accepts a later offer. The
Wolf can alter its manner in response to what Red visibly accepts or refuses,
but it cannot read what she has not revealed.

## Action questions

- What can Red verify with her own hand, stick, shoe, sight, and memory?
- Which part of the Wolf's assistance can she accept without granting it a
  claim over the journey?
- What does the Wolf mistake for welcome when Red uses a useful marker?
- How can the scene leave a trace that is useful now and potentially coercive
  later without making the present crossing impossible?
