# attention-trap-judgment — Netzach Scene Preparation

## Assignment identity

- **Episode:** `Script/Season_01/Episode_01`
- **Act:** 1
- **Scene identity:** `attention-trap-judgment`
- **Scene title:** Judgment Becomes Interrogation
- **Placement:** the rain-marked trace beyond the creek, after `crossing-conditional-help`
- **Readiness:** ready-scene assignment received
- **Assignment outline:** Red is moving toward grandmother with the covered basket when the Wolf interrupts with a judgment about her hood, youth, or home. She demands precision. The Wolf revises the judgment into narrower questions, appearing to listen while each answer gives it another opening. A minor practical difficulty permits genuinely useful attention, but Red verifies the observation and keeps the basket, distance, and direction under her control. The scene ends with her able to refuse and continue, while the Wolf's access and the safety of departure remain unresolved.

This preparation carries the accepted assignment into sensory and emotional
conditions. It does not write final script prose or determine either
character's private response. Red and the Wolf own their thoughts, speech, and
actions within the opportunities and constraints below.

## Continuity entering the scene

The immediately preceding performed scenes are `S1E1-Scene-01-Fork` and
`crossing-conditional-help` in `Script/Season_01/Episode_01/script.md`. Their
continuity notes establish:

- Red is beyond the fallen tree and the two-twig return mark, carrying the same
  covered basket along the rain-marked trace toward grandmother.
- She has crossed the washed-out creek with the provisions intact. The soaked
  branch remains behind as a route marker, and the three short marks remain at
  the creek; neither replaces the earlier mark.
- Late-day light is thinning after rain. Wet leaves, dripping branches, slick
  roots, bracken, and the trace's narrowing footing continue into this scene.
- Red has accepted useful information from the Wolf without granting it her
  basket, company, authority, or control of distance. She retains a hand free
  for testing and can resume her own pace.
- The Wolf was last left beside the creek after Red's departure, but ordinary
  offstage travel permits it to follow. It may know only what it has observed
  or what Red has said; its interpretation of bounded acceptance remains
  fallible.

The new interruption must therefore feel like an accumulation, not a reset:
the Wolf's previous useful guidance has earned attention, and Red's prior
boundary-setting has not made the path safe or the Wolf absent. The practical
difficulty in this scene should be small enough that Red can solve it herself,
but real enough that the Wolf can notice something before she does.

## Dramatic continuity

### Threads entering the scene

- **Red's discernment — red / #B33A3A:** She has already tested the fork,
  crossing, branch, and route marks. Here she tests language itself: a
  judgment must become precise before she will treat it as information.
- **The Wolf's enclosure — moss / #52634A:** It has learned that accurate
  observations can earn openings. It now tries to make Red's effort to correct
  it into a continuing obligation to answer, while preserving the appearance
  of responsiveness.
- **The unanswered threshold — amber / #B98532:** Red can recognize that the
  exchange is taking time and still lack a safe way to end it. Her ability to
  walk on remains visible; the cost of doing so while the Wolf is attentive
  remains unresolved.

### Change the audience must register

At entry, Red's attention is organized by the basket, the path, and the
distance to grandmother. The Wolf's first judgment interrupts that forward
rhythm. Each time Red asks, “What exactly do you mean?”, the Wolf should give a
more precise-sounding answer, but the answer should also move the question
closer to Red's private circumstances. By the end, Red has learned something
reliable about the Wolf's method and has protected a concrete boundary, yet
that discernment has cost steps, daylight, and attention. The audience should
hold both truths: she is not trapped by a spoken premise, and correcting the
premise has helped the Wolf remain in reach.

### Development decision: the physical rhythm

Use a four-part recurring rhythm, with each return slightly shorter and more
expensive:

1. Red walks; the Wolf names a judgment from a readable distance.
2. Red stops only enough to ask what the judgment means; the Wolf narrows it.
3. Red checks a concrete fact—the hood's tie, the basket's cover, a root, or
   the visible direction—and attempts to resume walking.
4. The Wolf accepts the correction in form, then asks the next narrower
   question before Red has fully regained her pace.

The third pass should include the minor practical difficulty: a strap, tie, or
cover has shifted against the basket while the ground slopes. The Wolf's notice
is genuinely useful, but Red can see and fix the problem herself. This prevents
the exchange from becoming only exposition and lets her test whether the
Wolf's help can remain bounded. The final pass should leave a clear gap in
which Red can proceed, refuse to answer, or hold the Wolf's question in the air;
the preparation must not choose that action for her.

## Sensory and performance vocabulary

### `<SCENE_HEADING>`

`ACT 1 — SCENE: JUDGMENT BECOMES INTERROGATION`, on the narrowing
rain-marked trace beyond the creek, late day after rain. The playing space
should show a route continuing toward grandmother and enough lateral room for
Red to maintain a chosen distance. The creek and its branch are behind her,
not erased; a damp stone, the sound of water, or a carried glance may preserve
their continuity without requiring exposition.

### `<TRANSITION>`

Carry the careful crossing rhythm into a renewed walking rhythm: one tested
step, then another, basket shifting softly, bracken brushing cloth. The Wolf's
arrival should interrupt a pace already underway, not stage a new meeting from
stillness. The exit should not resolve the interruption; it should return the
possibility of walking while leaving the Wolf's question and position active in
the space.

### `<CAMERA>`

If filmed, begin in a medium tracking view that keeps Red's hood, basket, feet,
and the route in one moving field. When the Wolf speaks, widen to preserve the
distance Red has chosen and include the Wolf's stillness or small repositioning
without making its face the answer. For each clarification, use a restrained
alternation between Red's speaking face or hands and the path she is trying to
recover; do not cut away from the time she loses. The practical difficulty
should be a close detail of the shifted tie, cover, or strap, followed by Red's
own check. At the end, target the open continuation of the trace and the space
between bodies, not a reaction shot that declares whether departure is safe.

### `<LIGHTING>`

Late-day light is cooler and thinner beneath the wet canopy. Patches of silver
on leaves and the red hood keep Red legible against the green-brown path, while
the Wolf remains fully readable in ordinary shade. Each pause allows the
audience to register that the light has moved a little farther toward evening;
no abrupt cue should make the Wolf's attention supernatural. If Red sets a
limit, the social warmth may cool through the same practical light, exposing
stillness, distance, and wet texture rather than changing the woods. The
closing image needs enough light to show a possible next step while making the
lost progress materially felt.

### `<AUDIO>`

Begin with footsteps on wet leaf litter, the basket's faint contents shifting,
residual drops, and the subdued creek receding behind them. The Wolf's voice
enters without a musical signal; its measured cadence should fit the woods
closely enough to seem attentive. Red's requests for precision create small
silences in which footfall stops, bracken settles, or a drop strikes the
basket's cover. Let the practical difficulty be heard through a soft scrape or
loosened fastening that Red can inspect. As the revisions accumulate, the
conversation should take over the rhythm that formerly belonged to walking.
At the close, restore one possible footstep under the unresolved question,
with no sound announcing escape, capture, or safety.

### `<ACTION>`

Red carries the basket throughout, checks its cover or fastening when the
ground shifts, and makes brief attempts to resume the route after each answer.
She may touch the hood's tie, indicate the path behind or ahead, count the
remaining daylight by looking through the trees, or keep one hand free. The
Wolf may hold position, angle its head, take one measured step that Red can
notice, or indicate the practical problem without touching Red or the basket.
The Wolf must not block the path, move Red, take her belongings, or convert the
small observation into proof that she needs it. The physical rhythm should let
the audience see access being accumulated through speech and placement rather
than through force.

### `<PARENTHETICAL>`

Use only playable conditions: *(still walking)*, *(at a distance)*,
*(checking the basket)*, *(touching the hood's tie)*, *(looking along the
trace)*, *(after a pause)*, *(indicating the loose fastening)*, *(trying to
continue)*. Do not assign private motives, fear, or realization. A pause may
be prepared as available space; the actors determine what it means.

### `<DIALOGUE>`

Red's available vocabulary is exact and concrete: what was said, what can be
seen, the basket, the hood, the path, home, grandmother, time, and distance.
Her questions can remain courteous while refusing the Wolf's premise. The
preparation should leave open whether she answers fully, withholds a detail,
or turns a question back toward observable ground. Her tone may shorten as
the exchange consumes the errand, but no single manner is prescribed.

The Wolf's public vocabulary is responsive and ordinary: “I meant,” “I only
noticed,” “you seem,” “is that what you carry,” and “where are you going?” It
should revise rather than openly contradict itself: judgment about youth can
become a claim about being unready; a comment about the hood can become a
question about who gave it to her; a comment about home can become a question
about who is waiting. The narrower wording must still be based on what the
Wolf could observe or what Red has revealed. Its questions may sound like an
invitation to correct the record while quietly asking Red to provide more of
the record. The Wolf's exact lines remain its authorship; its self-justifying
meaning must not be supplied by stage directions.

## Ordered beat preparation

### 1. `5171a01e981e:e1:b1` — judgment interrupts purposeful passage

- **Source content:** The Wolf interrupts Red's purposeful passage with a
  judgment about her hood, youth, or home, and Red requires it to say exactly
  what it means.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red's forward movement first; then the word or look
  that makes her stop. Keep the basket and the space between bodies visible so
  the interruption is an attention claim, not an announced threat.
- **Conditions:** The trace is passable but narrowing. The creek's residual
  sound is fading; late light remains sufficient for Red to continue. The Wolf
  can be heard or seen from a distance without having been invited closer.
- **Red context:** Objective: deliver the provisions through her own judgment.
  Her hood and home carry belonging, and her youth does not remove her right
  to the path. Preparation offers her a precise question and room to keep her
  basket and direction; her spoken response remains character-owned.
- **Wolf context:** Objective: make attention feel like concern and learn what
  kind of judgment Red will contest. It has only observable access to her hood,
  age as presented, manner, basket, and route. Its reading may be wrong.
- **Dramatic function:** Establishes the interruption as a social problem Red
  chooses to examine, while preserving a visible route she could try to take.

### 2. `5171a01e981e:e1:b2` — responsive narrowing becomes a cost

- **Source content:** The Wolf revises its judgment into narrower questions,
  presenting responsiveness as listening while Red tests each claim against
  the path, the basket, and what she can observe.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** The repeated pattern of question, answer, and
  attempted resumption. Each revision should appear to repair the previous
  imprecision while moving closer to Red's personal circumstances.
- **Conditions:** Red can take one or two steps between exchanges. The Wolf's
  position should remain readable and non-blocking, though a small adjustment
  of space may make the route feel narrower without changing its geography.
- **Red context:** She can decide that precision protects her from being judged
  by a false premise, while also noticing that every correction requires more
  of her time and information. Withholding a detail, naming a contradiction,
  or answering only the observable part must remain available.
- **Wolf context:** It treats each answer as ground gained and may mistake
  Red's wish to correct it for welcome of continued attention. Its apparent
  listening is tactical but need not be consciously exposed in speech. It must
  revise from evidence rather than mind-read.
- **Dramatic function:** Makes the mechanism legible: responsiveness can be a
  form of access. Red's discernment improves while the errand's forward rhythm
  begins to belong to the exchange.

### 3. `5171a01e981e:e1:b3` — practical observation without purchased closeness

- **Source content:** A practical difficulty briefly gives the Wolf an excuse
  to offer help, but Red keeps hold of the basket or her route so the offer
  cannot fully purchase closeness.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** The shifted fastening, cover, or strap; Red's own
  verification; then the distance that remains after the Wolf's useful notice.
- **Conditions:** A slope, root, or brush has loosened the basket's cover or
  tie enough to risk a small spill, but not enough to create injury or crisis.
  The Wolf can see the problem from where it stands. Red can correct it
  herself, and the path remains physically open.
- **Red context:** The observation may be genuinely useful. She can accept the
  information without handing over the basket, inviting touch, or agreeing to
  company. Her objective remains the delivery; her hidden wish to be trusted
  with the errand may make the distinction emotionally active without fixing
  her response.
- **Wolf context:** It wants the useful notice to count as evidence that its
  presence is wanted. It may offer to come nearer, carry something, or explain
  how it knows, but no offer is accepted by the preparation. Its favor remains
  conditional only if its observable treatment makes it so.
- **Dramatic function:** Separates real practical value from entitlement. Red's
  bounded acceptance can be a success for her and still teach the Wolf the
  wrong lesson about access.

### 4. `5171a01e981e:e1:b4` — return to the errand becomes difficult

- **Source content:** Red attempts to end the exchange and return to
  grandmother, measuring the daylight and distance she has lost while the Wolf
  adjusts its warmth and position.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red's first full attempt to walk on, the route ahead,
  and the visible change in the Wolf's warmth, timing, or spatial claim. The
  audience should feel that the woods have not changed, but her experience of
  them has.
- **Conditions:** The light is lower, the trace remains wet and slick, and the
  Wolf can speak from behind or alongside without blocking. Red has lost
  measurable steps and time, not her ability to move.
- **Red context:** She may check the basket, look toward the next stretch, or
  make the destination explicit as a way to close the encounter. The effort of
  watching the Wolf's response competes with care for grandmother. Her exact
  threshold for ending the conversation remains open.
- **Wolf context:** A limit threatens its newly won access. It can become
  warmer, cooler, more practical, or more wounded in manner, but it remains
  responsible for the treatment it chooses. It may present Red's wish to leave
  as impatience or ingratitude without establishing that this is true.
- **Dramatic function:** Converts the cost of attention into visible lost
  progress and prepares the pivotal turn: Red's pursuit of a clean correction
  will itself offer the Wolf another question.

  and the Wolf has no physical right to stop her. The distance between them
  must remain usable, though socially expensive to cross or maintain.
- **Red context:** Her discernment can now include the method of the exchange:
  correction does not end the judgment; it refines the Wolf's next reach. She
  may refuse the premise, stop answering, continue walking, or give one last
  concrete distinction. No one response is prescribed, and recognizing the
  pattern does not supply a safe exit.
- **Wolf context:** It has learned from what Red actually said and observed,
  but its conclusion that access is deserved remains fallible. Its objective
  is to keep the encounter feeling voluntary while making Red's own demand

  the audience should infer its pressure from behavior, not receive an
  authorial confession.
- **Dramatic function:** Carries the season's attention-cost thread forward.
  The Wolf's judgment has become an interrogation without becoming a physical
  trap; Red's right to refuse and proceed remains present, while the cost of
  exercising that right has become the scene's unresolved danger.
- **Exit condition:** The scene ends with Red still able to choose a next step,
  refuse the Wolf's question, or hold her ground. The Wolf's access is not

## Handoff responsibilities

Later scene-writing, roundtable, performance-reference, and revision phases
must preserve the ordered local beat IDs, scene identity, episode ownership,
and pivotal identity `BEAT-G6-3-1` with every predecessor link listed above.
They should carry forward the covered basket, wet-marked trace, receding creek
sound, thinning light, the Wolf's prior useful route knowledge, the practical
fastening or cover check, and the measurable loss of walking time. The exact
judgment, questions, pauses, gestures, and final choice belong to Red and the
Wolf in performance.

The scene must not establish mind-reading, magical compulsion, transformation,
a device that settles the conflict, or a completed danger. The Wolf may invent
or overstate a social or woodland claim, but the world does not certify it by
itself. Red's attention, distance, basket, and direction remain materially
hers, even as the encounter makes them harder to protect. The final image
should preserve an open path and an unresolved question rather than a resolved
interpretation.
