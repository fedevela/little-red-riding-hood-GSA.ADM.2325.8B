# Dramatic Action Brief — Judgment Becomes Interrogation

## Scene frame

This is one bounded encounter on the rain-marked trace beyond the creek, late in
the day. Red enters still moving toward grandmother with the covered basket;
the fork, two-twig return mark, blocked road, creek branch, and three short
creek marks remain behind her. The route ahead is open but wet, root-lined,
and partly hidden by bracken. The Wolf can be encountered after ordinary travel
and must remain at a readable, non-blocking distance. No capture, rescue,
escape, reconciliation, magical compulsion, or completed danger is established.

The physical rhythm should make access accumulate without making the outcome
automatic: continuous footfalls become a stop, a precise question, one or two
recoverable steps, another question, and then shorter attempts to resume. Red
can answer, withhold, refuse the premise, test what is said, or continue. The
Wolf can revise, soften, cool, wait, or ask again, but its reading of Red is
fallible and its usefulness does not create entitlement.

## Knowledge and continuity

Red knows the purpose and distance of her errand, the finite daylight, and the
concrete conditions she can inspect. She knows the Wolf has previously supplied
partly useful terrain information and that accepting a fact did not grant it
authority. She does not know the Wolf's private hunger. Her concealed concern
is that managing the Wolf's response may cost the journey; she need not disclose
that concern.

The Wolf knows the woods and can observe Red's hood, basket, footing, direction,
and disclosed answers. It learns only what observation or speech makes
available. It wants to convert attention into influence while preserving the
appearance of voluntary exchange. It may mistake a correction, pause, or
courtesy for welcome. The basket remains Red's; the path remains hers to pursue;
the Wolf's position may make the route feel narrower without physically closing
it.

There are no additional established non-speaking participants in this scene.
The woods, wet ground, bracken, fading light, basket fastening, and residual
creek sound are environmental conditions rather than characters with authored
intentions.

## Dramatic progression

The Wolf's apparent responsiveness should be legible in small repairs rather
than explanatory speeches. Red's precision should produce real gains: each
answer distinguishes what can be seen from what the Wolf is presuming. The
cost is equally concrete: a lost step, a delayed arrival, a shorter remaining
light, a hand diverted to the basket, and attention turned from grandmother to
the Wolf's mood. The practical difficulty offers the Wolf a genuine, limited
way to help; Red's own verification keeps that usefulness from purchasing
touch, transfer of the basket, or chosen closeness. The final condition is an
open route beside an unanswered narrower question.
