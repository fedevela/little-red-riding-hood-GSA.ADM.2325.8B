# crossing-conditional-help — Netzach Scene Preparation

## Assignment identity

- **Episode:** `Script/Season_01/Episode_01`
- **Act:** 1
- **Scene identity:** `crossing-conditional-help`
- **Scene title:** The Marked Crossing
- **Placement:** continuation of Red's movement beyond the wet-marked trace
- **Readiness:** ready-scene assignment received
- **Assignment outline:** At a washed-out creek crossing, Red encounters unstable footing, a soaked branch, and a basket whose provisions must remain protected. The Wolf identifies stable stones and bends the branch toward a narrow fork. Red tests the information, crosses with the basket and destination intact, and leaves with the route still her own. The Wolf reads this bounded acceptance as an opening to make later progress depend on its assistance.

This preparation carries the accepted assignment into sensory and emotional
conditions. It does not write final script prose or determine either
character's private response. Red and the Wolf own their thoughts, speech, and
actions within the opportunities and constraints below.

## Continuity entering the scene

The immediately preceding performed scene is `S1E1-Scene-01-Fork` in
`Script/Season_01/Episode_01/script.md`. Its continuity notes establish:

- Red is already carrying the provisions into the wet-marked trace after rain,
  with the basket's cover secured and one hand available for testing.
- The two-twig return mark remains at the fork behind her. The fallen tree still
  blocks the ordinary road, and the last light is narrowing.
- Red chose the trace and its direction herself. The Wolf's route information
  was partly useful, but Red did not grant it her basket, company, authority, or
  control of distance.
- The Wolf remained near the road edge and did not physically redirect Red.
  It can know only what it observes or what Red has said; its interpretation of
  her choice remains fallible.

The crossing therefore inherits a journey already made more difficult by the
Wolf's attention. The water obstacle is new physical information, not a reset
of the earlier fork. The audience should be able to remember the mark behind
Red even when the stage no longer displays it.

## Dramatic continuity and development decision

### Threads entering

- **Red's discernment — red / #B33A3A:** Her first test was the trace and its
  roots. Here she must distinguish a useful physical observation from the
  larger claim that the Wolf should become her guide.
- **The Wolf's enclosure — moss / #52634A:** The Wolf has learned that accurate,
  bounded help can earn attention without openly demanding closeness. It now
  makes the help more material: a branch can point toward safety, and its
  absence later can be made to feel like a loss.
- **The unanswered threshold — amber / #B98532:** The crossing is passable,
  not safe. Red's successful step proves only this patch of ground; it does not
  settle what follows or whether the Wolf's knowledge deserves trust.

### Concrete practical favor

The Wolf identifies a line of stable stones beneath the brown water and bends a
soaked branch so that its fork rests visibly toward the first and last reliable
footings. The branch is genuinely useful: it gives Red a handhold and a visual
sequence for this crossing while keeping the basket out of the water. It is
also a visible trace of Wolf-mediated orientation. Red can use the branch and
then leave it behind, or acknowledge the information without surrendering her
route. The branch must not become a magical guide, a lock, or a device that
settles the conflict.

The Wolf's first dependence-making move is therefore not that Red cannot cross
without it. It is that the Wolf notices how easily a single accurate marker can
become an invitation to ask for the next one. Later assistance can be framed as
necessary because the Wolf has begun placing itself between Red and what she
can see. The audience should see the seed of this plan while Red's bounded
acceptance remains materially successful.

### Change the audience must register

At entry, Red is carrying forward a choice she made without the Wolf. At the
creek, the Wolf can make one immediate problem easier. By exit, Red has crossed
with her basket, destination, pace, and judgment intact, yet the Wolf has found
a way to make its knowledge look like part of the route itself. Assistance has
become a possible condition without becoming a completed claim.

## Sensory and performance vocabulary

### `<SCENE_HEADING>`

`ACT 1 — SCENE: THE MARKED CROSSING`, a washed-out creek within the rain-marked
trace, late day after rain. The playing space should retain a visible direction
back toward the fork even if the two-twig mark is now distant or only carried in
Red's memory. The creek is a threshold made by water, unstable stones, and the
choice of where to place a body.

### `<TRANSITION>`

Carry the careful rhythm from the roots into a halt at running water. Red's
forward movement is interrupted by an obstacle that is narrower than the fallen
tree but more immediate to the body. The transition out is a renewed crossing
rhythm: one tested stone, then another, with no triumphant clearing of danger.

### `<CAMERA>`

If filmed, begin in a medium tracking view that keeps Red, basket, and the
wet-marked route together, then settle on water, the soaked branch, and the
stones beneath the surface. Introduce the Wolf in a wide two-shot preserving
Red's chosen distance. When the branch is bent, target the branch, the Wolf's
paw, and the available stones in the same visual field; do not make the Wolf's
face the answer to the crossing. Follow Red's foot and basket across the water.
The closing target is the branch left angled behind her and the next stretch of
path, not a look that confirms the Wolf's private plan.

### `<LIGHTING>`

Late-day light is cooler beneath the wet canopy. Water catches broken silver;
the stones are visible only in intervals where the surface clears. The soaked
branch should be dark against the water and legible once angled. The Wolf may
remain in readable shade, never requiring a supernatural change in light. Red's
crossing needs enough illumination for the audience to see that she tests and
chooses each footing, while the dimming surroundings make delay costly.

### `<AUDIO>`

Use the residual drops, low creek run, wet leaves, and careful footfalls
inherited from the trace. The basket's provisions should shift softly when Red
balances. A branch flexing and water striking stone make the Wolf's practical
intervention audible. After Red accepts only the crossing information, leave a
brief space in which creek and breath replace conversational warmth. The exit
sound is one stable step after another, with water continuing underneath; no
cue should announce safety or reveal the Wolf's plan.

### `<ACTION>`

The creek gives the performers concrete business: Red protects the basket,
tests a bank and stone, checks the branch's stability, and chooses whether to
touch it. The Wolf may indicate submerged stones and bend the branch from its
own side or from a position Red can clearly observe. It must not carry the
basket, catch Red, place her feet, or block the bank. Red can cross while
keeping her pace and direction. The branch remains a trace that can be seen,
used, and left behind.

### `<PARENTHETICAL>`

Keep parentheticals playable: *(at the bank)*, *(testing the stone)*,
*(indicating the branch)*, *(holding the basket clear)*, *(after a pause)*,
*(from the far side)*. Do not assign private feelings or state that the Wolf's
help is coercive as an inner fact. Observable changes in distance, timing,
voice, and willingness to answer are sufficient.

### `<DIALOGUE>`

Red's available vocabulary is concrete and verifiable: water, stones, branch,
weight, basket, bank, and the direction of the trace. Her courtesy can accept
an observation while limiting what the Wolf is invited to do. The destination
remains Grandmother's home, carried through purpose rather than exposition.

The Wolf can speak in the language of saving time, keeping things dry, and
knowing where the creek deepens. It may treat Red's practical acceptance as
evidence that its presence is welcome, but this is its fallible reading, not a
fact the scene grants. Its later dependence-making intention should be visible
through the offer's shape and the attention it pays to the marker, not through
authorial explanation in dialogue.

## Ordered beat preparation

### 1. `070275abd30b:e1:b1` — the damaged crossing

- **Source content:** The crossing has been damaged, leaving unstable footing,
  a soaked branch, and a basket whose provisions must remain protected.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red's basket and hands first, then the water's
  uncertain surface and the route continuing beyond it.
- **Conditions:** Recent rain has widened or loosened the creek. The bank is
  passable enough to inspect, but the first step cannot be taken casually.
- **Red context:** Objective: keep the provisions moving without surrendering
  judgment. Her subtext and tone remain open to her authorship; the scene gives
  her time to observe before answering anyone.
- **Wolf context:** If present or heard, it has access only to Red's visible
  difficulty and the terrain it knows. Its objective and private reading remain
  character-owned.
- **Dramatic function:** Converts the earlier route choice into an immediate
  bodily problem and creates a legitimate occasion for limited assistance.

### 2. `070275abd30b:e1:b2` — precise, bounded guidance

- **Source content:** The Wolf identifies stable stones and angles a branch
  toward a narrow fork, offering knowledge that addresses the immediate
  obstacle without claiming Red's destination.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** The line of stones, the branch's altered angle, and
  the distance between the two characters.
- **Conditions:** The stones are genuinely firmer, but the Wolf cannot promise
  every step beyond this crossing. The branch can serve as a marker and a
  handhold but is soaked and imperfect.
- **Red context:** She can ask for sequence or evidence, compare the claim with
  the water, and decide how much of the information to use. Accepting route
  knowledge does not require accepting the Wolf's proximity.
- **Wolf context:** Objective: make its knowledge useful enough to earn another
  opening. It may present the favor as ordinary competence while watching what
  Red does with it; the scene does not establish what Red privately believes.
- **Dramatic function:** Repeats the earlier pattern at a higher material cost:
  the Wolf is right about something, which makes future conditional help more
  credible and more dangerous.

### 3. `070275abd30b:e1:b3` — bounded acceptance

- **Source content:** Red checks the Wolf's directions against visible water
  and stones, accepts only the route information, and keeps the basket and pace
  under her control.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red's test before contact, the basket remaining with
  her, and the Wolf's body staying outside the decision.
- **Conditions:** The water provides evidence but not certainty. Red can use
  the branch or stones without letting the Wolf take over the crossing.
- **Red context:** Her objective is to cross while preserving the right to set
  pace and distance. A pause, qualification, refusal of further help, or
  practical thanks are all available expressions rather than prescribed lines.
- **Wolf context:** It observes acceptance of information, not consent to
  company or control. Its reading may nevertheless inflate the significance of
  Red's use of the marker.
- **Dramatic function:** Makes Red's agency visible inside cooperation and gives
  the Wolf a real but limited success from which it can draw the wrong lesson.

### 4. `070275abd30b:e1:b4` — the marker becomes a watchpoint

- **Source content:** Red crosses and makes an independent mark of the route,
  while the Wolf remains close enough to present its knowledge as protection
  and watches for evidence that its help has become necessary.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red's independent mark and the branch left behind;
  then the Wolf's watchfulness in relation to both.
- **Conditions:** Red reaches the far bank with provisions intact. The mark is
  modest and fallible, not a guarantee or a device that resolves danger. The
  Wolf has not physically followed into her space unless the performance
  authorizes a clearly observed, non-blocking position.
- **Red context:** She can record the crossing for herself and continue toward
  the destination. The Wolf's framing of its knowledge as protection may invite
  an answer, but she is not required to validate that framing.
- **Wolf context:** It can observe that the branch and stones helped Red cross;
  it cannot know that she now depends on it. Its attention to the mark is the
  beginning of a plan, not proof that the plan will work.
- **Dramatic function:** Leaves a visible continuity object for later scenes and
  gives the Wolf a concrete way to imagine itself necessary without making that
  imagination true.

### 5. `070275abd30b:e1:b5` — bounded success becomes a plan

- **Source content:** Red uses the Wolf's marked guidance to cross the damaged
  creek while retaining her basket, destination, and judgment, and the Wolf
  converts that bounded success into a plan for making future progress depend
  on its help.

- **Audience attention:** Hold both facts together: Red arrives on the far
  bank under her own control, and the Wolf remains studying what its marker has

- **Conditions:** The crossing is complete only for this patch of water.

## Handoff responsibilities

Later scene-writing, roundtable, and revision phases must preserve the ordered
local beat IDs, scene identity, episode ownership, and pivotal identity
`BEAT-G3-3-1` with every predecessor link listed above. They should carry
forward the washed-out creek, submerged stones, soaked branch, Red's basket,
the independent route mark, the continuing late-day light, and the Wolf's
fallible interpretation.

The performed scene must not establish mind-reading, magical compulsion,
transformation, a device that settles the conflict, or a resolved outcome. It
must preserve Red's dignity and the Wolf's responsibility. The audience should
leave seeing a real favor and the first visible attempt to make that favor a
condition of future movement, while Red's route, fate, and the eventual
threshold remain open.
