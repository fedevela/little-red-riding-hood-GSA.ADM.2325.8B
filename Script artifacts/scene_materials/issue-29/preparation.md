# Phase 5 — Netzach: Preparation

**Scene:** The Departure — Red Sets Out
**Issue:** 29
**Episode:** Script/Season_01/Episode_01
**Act:** 1

---

## Placement and Continuity

This is the opening scene of Season 1. No earlier performed text precedes it;
no continuity notes exist to carry forward. Everything established here becomes
the first entry in the season's dramatic record. The Wolf has not yet appeared.
Red is alone.

---

## Environmental Conditions

**Light**

Early morning in mixed woodland. The first light is already down and working —
not dawn haze, not full midday brightness, but the particular quality of a
summer or late-spring morning hour when the sun is low enough that it angles
through the canopy rather than beating on it. Birch trunks catch it and scatter
it; beech crowns absorb it and soften it. The effect is strips and intervals:
brightness, then filtered green-grey, then brightness again. The path itself
lies in alternating light and shade.

As Red moves deeper — birch thinning into older trees — the strips go from
nearly vertical to horizontal, long flat lines of particulate light catching
dust and insects. The transition marks the shift in scene register. Closer to
the garden gate the world feels more legible. Deeper in, the light thickens
and goes quiet.

Overhead, cloud cover moves. At the fork, a cloud shifts and the sun reading
becomes less reliable. This is the moment uncertainty has a physical cause.

**Sound**

Morning woodland ambient: birdsong without urgency, the occasional creak of
upper branches in a slight breeze, the soft irregular percussion of footfall on
a path that alternates between packed earth and sections gone soft with ground
moisture. The broth jar does not rattle if the cork is sound; the cloth over
the bread muffles any shift of contents. Red's passage is quieter than she
thinks — which will make the sound she notices behind her, near the end, carry
more weight when it arrives.

The ambient falls just short of silent. It is the kind of quiet that can be
read as peaceful or as listening.

**Setting and Path**

The path originates at a garden gate and runs through tended-to-wild transition
before entering the woods proper. The garden gate is practical metal or weathered
wood — functional, not decorative. Its latch catches reliably and makes a sound
when it does.

From the gate: a short open stretch, then the stand of birch. The fallen beech
is known territory — Red navigates the left-step around the mud pool from
memory, without thought. This is the last familiar landmark before the path
enters terrain she has only partly traveled.

The fork comes earlier than her mental map predicted. Two paths, both showing
wear; the distinction between them is ambiguous at the margin. The left-path
mud shows more churning at the edge — foot traffic or animal passage, unclear.
The sun is not cooperative for compass reading at this moment.

Twenty steps past the fork, the ground goes soft — groundwater held just under
the surface. The path edge where she steps to avoid it carries an exposed root
at ankle height.

Further in: older trees, horizontal light, the path going quieter underfoot.
This is where the private thought arrives. It is also the place from which she
could not easily know if something had entered the path behind her.

**Physical Conditions and Props**

The basket is carried in the left hand primarily, switched to the right under
load. It is genuinely heavy — bread plus a corked jar of broth, wrapped
carefully. The warmth of the bread is tactile through the cloth.

The red hood is on her shoulders. It arrived from home; it travels with her.
She is not performing it. It belongs to her in the way familiar things do —
not as a symbol she is conscious of carrying, but as part of what it feels
like to be herself.

Sturdy shoes for woodland use. By the stumble, the sole of at least one shoe
carries soft-path mud. She does not remark on this aloud.

## Immediate Pressures

**Errand obligation as weight.** The basket is the yes she already gave. She
said she would go; grandmother is waiting; the bread is warm right now and will
be cooler later. Time is being spent in increments she cannot recover. The errand
is not a pretext — it is real care for someone she loves, and that realness
gives every hesitation a cost.

**The question running ahead of her feet.** She is not only completing an errand.
She needs to know whether the judgment required to complete it belongs to her.
The question — *Is this mine?* — is not formed yet as a question she has named;
it arrives as a pressure, a checking-ahead of where her feet are. The walk and
the private inquiry are simultaneous.

**The mother's ambiguity.** *You know the way well enough.* Heard two ways at
once: confidence and qualification. Red left before resolving which was meant.
That unresolved reading travels with her and will shape how she measures her
own performance against some absent audience.

**The fork.** A navigational decision without an authoritative instruction. She
has to weigh partial evidence — her compass sense, the state of the mud, the
curves of both paths — and commit. The decision is real; being wrong has a cost.

**The stumble and its double wish.** Catching the root without falling is a
recovery. It is private. She wants that recovery witnessed *and* the stumble
unseen, and those two wants are incompatible. This moment seeds the deeper
theme: the gap between the internal account (what actually happened) and the
external account (what she can claim). She cannot have a clean story; she can
have the truth of having recovered.

**The shrinking morning.** She is using time she cannot get back. Each decision
point — the gate, the fork, the stumble — spends daylight. She is not late
yet, but the margin is not infinite. The errand has a deadline that doesn't
announce itself loudly but is present in every pause.

**The sound at the end.** A sound behind her — probably nothing. She turns and
looks. Nothing visible. She keeps walking. The woods have become the kind of
place where she notices that category of sound. This is the last beat of the
scene. The Wolf has not appeared. But the scene ends with her having attended
to the space behind her.

## Sensory Vocabulary

These images and physical registers are available for the scene's expression.
They do not prescribe action; they name the field the character inhabits.

- **Hands and arms:** The weight of the basket migrating from hands to shoulder
  to neck over the walk's duration. Switching hands. Adjusting grip. The bread's
  warmth through cloth. The cork checked twice before she left.
- **Feet and ground:** Earth that is packed, then unexpectedly soft. The specific
  sensation of avoiding a known mud pool by memory versus stepping around an
  unknown wet patch. The lurch of a root caught too late to avoid, too soon to
  arrest cleanly.
- **Eyes and light:** Looking up to read the sun through canopy and failing to get
  a clean compass read. The strips of light and shadow marking the path's
  character as she walks deeper. The long horizontal lines of light in the older
  trees.
- **Body posture and self-watching:** Shoulders that pull back, stride that
  lengthens — movement performed for no visible audience and noticed as such.
  The small bodily claim of having committed to the left path. The recognition
  that these adjustments are a kind of performance, and that the performance has
  no viewer.
- **Inward candor:** Embarrassment at reading the latch as an omen. Ironic
  self-commentary at the stumble (*Graceful. Nailed it.*). The private speech
  she makes at the fork and says to no one. The thought she files away rather
  than examining — *think about later.*
- **Time and cost:** Daylight as pocket money. The morning being used in
  non-recoverable increments.

## Source-Grounded Character Context

**Red's pursuit** (from [`objective.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/objective.md)):
Deliver the provisions through her own judgment. Care makes the errand real;
independence makes it personally important. The right to choose company and
distance belongs to the journey she hopes to make.

**Red's hidden wish** (from [`hidden_objective.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/hidden_objective.md)):
To arrive with her own account of the journey intact: what she welcomed, what
troubled her, and what she chose. She wants the internal and external versions
to match. She is less certain of her competence than she wants a stranger to
see — and keeps that uncertainty close.

**Self-conflict** (from [`conflict_with_self.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/conflict_with_self.md)):
She may give another's confident explanation more time than her own unease. The
wish to seem capable can make admitting uncertainty difficult. She values
independence yet may still look outward for confirmation. If she refuses help
she risks feeling foolish; if she accepts it she risks surrendering judgment.

**Environmental conflict** (from [`conflict_with_environment.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/conflict_with_environment.md)):
The woodland path asks for attention to distance, footing, and provisions.
Home supplies familiar rules; out here she must judge what those rules cannot
settle. Woodland uncertainty leaves room for another creature to make obedience
sound like the price of finding her way.

**Voice and register** (from [`line_of_thought.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/line_of_thought.md)):
Short bursts, fragments, quick pivots, self-corrections. Light slang and meme
phrasing when it serves an immediate reaction. Private candor runs ahead of
spoken courtesy. Ironic understatement saves face. Humor can come out too
brightly, fail to land, or be abandoned. Inwardly she is sharper than she
speaks aloud.

**Images** (from [`line_of_images.md`](../../../bible/characters/little_red_riding_hood/line_of_images.md)):
Red cloth as nearness of home. The basket as care with weight. The path as both
direction and room to choose the next step. The thought of arriving carries
grandmother and the hope of telling the journey in her own words.

**Arc context** (from [`dramatic_arcs.md`](../../../bible/dramatic_arcs.md)):
Red's discernment arc begins here. Her wish to be capable is meeting the first
real test before any external pressure from the Wolf. The gap the season will
widen — between performed competence and felt competence — opens in these first
minutes alone. What the Wolf will find when it enters is a character already
engaged in a private argument with herself about whether the judgment she is
exercising belongs to her.

## Open Character Questions for Phase 6

These questions belong to Red's own expression and remain open for the beats.
Phase 6 will order them; the roundtable is their lived resolution.

1. At what exact moment does the private question (*Is this mine?*) surface from
   the errand's weight — before the gate is latched, while the birch trunks are
   still ahead, or not until the familiar landmarks run out?

2. How does she hold the ambiguity of *well enough* — does she push it aside
   immediately, return to it at the fork, or carry it as a quiet measuring stick
   she keeps checking her performance against?

3. What does she notice about herself when she catches the posture adjustment —
   the shoulders-back, stride-lengthened performance for no audience? How long
   does she let herself look at it before filing it away?

4. At the fork, what is the quality of the decision she makes? Is it more like
   a problem she solves, a bet she places, or a commitment she performs? The
   difference will determine what the choice costs her before the Wolf arrives
   to offer an easier alternative.

5. The stumble and its double wish — wanting the recovery seen and the stumble
   unseen simultaneously — is the clearest moment of the scene's central tension.
   Does she arrive at this contradiction with any humor, or is it just a flash of
   something uncomfortable she keeps walking through?

6. By the time she is in the older trees and thinking about arriving: how clean
   is the story she wants to tell? What words does she try on? Which ones sit
   wrong, and why?

7. The sound behind her: how long does she stand still before deciding it is
   nothing? Does she speak to herself during that pause, or only after she has
   turned and found nothing?

---

## What This Scene Establishes for the Season

The architecture of Red's self-trust — its sources, its gaps, its pressure
points — is fully visible before the Wolf arrives. The errand is real; the
private wish is real; the uncertainty is real. The Wolf will find a character
who is already alert to the difference between performing competence and
exercising it, already aware that the external account and the internal one may
not match, already practicing the habit of keeping her doubts private while she
decides what they mean.

The sound at the end places the season's question on the path without naming it.
Something is behind her. Probably nothing. The question travels forward with her.
