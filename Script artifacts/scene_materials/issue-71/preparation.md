## Placement and Continuity

This is Scene 1 of Act 1 of Season 1, Episode 2 — the first scene of the second episode, the fourth attempt by the Wolf, and the opening of the act the parent issue names *The Invented Danger*. The complete performed text of Episode 1 (issues 67, 68, 69, and 70) is preserved in [`prior_performance.md`](prior_performance.md) in this directory. No state established by those scenes is reset; this scene begins inside their accumulated physical and social consequences.

**What Red carries in from Episode 1:**

The basket is in her left hand — the errand hand — as it was at the close of Scene 4 (Episode 1, Act 2, Scene 2). The bread is cool: the warmth it had at the gate has been gone for some time. The jar beneath the cloth is as it has always been, heavier and cooler, ordinary. The errand's clock has been running the full length of Episode 1 and continues now.

The wet right shoe has been a background fact since Scene 1, Beat 3 of Episode 1. Neither resolved nor mentioned since. Still there, carrying the morning through it.

**The birchwood inconsistency (evidentiary item, Episode 1 Act 1, Scene 2):** The Wolf named the birchwood fork as the turning for the *far houses* before Red had disclosed her destination. She registered this as a fractional grip-tightening (Episode 1, Beat 6 of Scene 2) and kept walking. It has never been spoken aloud. It retains the texture of something testable.

**The endearment-and-cooling (atmospheric item, Episode 1 Act 2, Scene 1):** The Wolf's offer of company arrived with *honey* mid-clause. After her refusal, the warmth returned shorter — the warmth of competence present but the warmth of claimed intimacy absent. She registered the rhythm change. She has not brought this as an accusation.

**The observation (Episode 1 Act 2, Scene 2):** The Wolf named what it had watched across two terrain encounters: *You look before you step. Not everyone bothers with that.* She let the warmth in because what was named was real — her attentiveness, the specific quality her hidden objective aches to have recognized. The observation demonstrated what it claimed; she could not hold it at arm's length without denying herself. The extension that followed — *Path gets softer past the old growth. Good to have eyes on it that pay attention* — placed the Wolf in the journey's continuation without asking permission. She did not answer the extension directly. The moment passed before she found the form for refusal; she noticed the moment had passed and did not go back for it.

**Her inventory at this scene's opening:**

Three items in private inventory, none voiced, carried alongside the basket since their respective moments:
1. The birchwood inconsistency: evidentiary — a specific fact that does not fit the Wolf's presentation, testable in principle. The texture of something she could bring into the light.
2. The endearment-and-cooling: atmospheric — a felt wrongness in a rhythm change. Difficult to make legible outside her own register.
3. The extension from Episode 1, Scene 4: relational — a social presumption that took warmth as permission. *He placed himself in my future* does not carry what she perceived outside her own register. These two items, different in kind from the first, do not yet speak to each other.

**The direction she accepted proved accurate:** The Wolf's sequential terrain instructions through the birchwood and past the root-crossing cluster all landed correctly. Its practical credibility has been confirmed by her body. This is the ground on which its next approach will be built.

**Her humor's current availability:** Quieter than it was at the gate, with less room in it than it had through most of Episode 1. The extension's unanswered weight has contracted the quip-pattern further. Not absent; available, if the occasion is right and the attentional cost is manageable.

**The Wolf's entering position:** It recalibrated following the observation's partial success in Episode 1. The non-answer to the extension was filed as tacit acceptance. Its next approach is built on that misreading: it believes the relationship can absorb a refusal and recover, and that accurate observation is an effective instrument. Its confidence in its own expertise-claim is intact — it has not yet had that authority tested. That assumption is wrong, and the testing begins in this scene.

## Geography and Setting

Episode 1 ended with the canopy beginning its far thinning at the edge of sight. This scene opens in the terrain that follows: the old growth has thinned, the earth past the canopy line gives differently underfoot, as the Wolf predicted it would in Episode 1's closing exchange. The path is softer here — not treacherous, but with a yielding quality the packed old-growth path did not have. The feeling underfoot is new-ish: she recognized it approaching, noticed the quality of difference the Wolf described before it was fully underfoot, the body receiving the predicted information.

**The ford, its placement, and its memory:**

The ford lies behind her. She crossed it some distance back, before the second bend in the path. The crossing was unremarkable: one careful step from the bank onto the first crossing-stone — her hand went briefly to the basket handle before settling back — the near bank firm underfoot, ordinary, the approach entirely normal. No give in the bank, no silt buildup requiring navigation, no particular condition worth managing. The water-crossing was a fact she handled and continued past.

The second bend is also behind her. She has taken both bends. The path she is on now is past the second turning, the old growth opened behind her, the terrain ahead holding the quality of the next section of the route to grandmother's house.

**The immediate topography:**

The thinned canopy admits more sky than the old-growth section. The light has changed character: the flat horizontal old-tree planes have given way to something more open, the autumn morning sky directly available in patches. The quality of sound is different too — the acoustic shelter of the old wood is behind her; birdsong arrives with slightly more direction and definition here, not muffled by dense canopy. The path surface is the notable feature: softer underfoot than the packed old-growth earth, with the particular yielding quality that follows ground in the open stretch. Not muddy, not treacherous. Just different in how it receives a step.

**Light:**

The morning has advanced. The low sideways angle of the gate-opening is far past; the sun has climbed to a flatter autumn angle that is still clearly morning but no longer the quality of first light. In the thinned canopy section, this light is available more directly — patches of direct autumn sun on the path surface, longer shadows from the remaining trees, the overall register softer than the dappled alternation of the long straight in Episode 1. The path ahead can be read clearly; there are no optical ambiguities to navigate. The light is not dramatic. It is simply the morning continued.

**Sound:**

The ambient is more open here than in the old-growth shelter. Birdsong with direction — individual birds registrable rather than the diffuse canopy-cover acoustic of the older trees. The occasional distant sound of upper branches in the wood behind carries into this space differently, with more air around it. The Wolf's voice in this setting does not require any elevated projection; it sounds the way it sounds in the ordinary air between two figures on a path.

## The Wolf's Position and Entering Condition

The Wolf is present. They are walking together — or in close proximity on the path, the Wolf maintaining the position its extension placed it in at the close of Episode 1. Its manner is continuous with what has been established: unhurried, practical, the warm competence that has been accurate and therefore carries earned credibility. The rebuilt warmth from Episode 1's closing scene is in its posture and cadence rather than in any gesture.

What is new in the Wolf's entering condition: it has been watching since Episode 1's observation. Its revision from transactional (offer bundled with company) to observational (naming what is true about her) proved partially effective — the warmth landed, the extension went unanswered rather than refused. Its inventory from that scene: she accepts what is accurate, she held the practical parts of every offer, and she did not vocalize suspicion. Its read on the non-answer is more permission than she granted. This misreading will shape the approach it now makes.

The Wolf's chosen instrument for the fourth attempt is specificity. Not a general warning, not vague concern — a particular hazard, in a particular place, described with the particular authority of a creature that has walked this terrain many times. The same authority that provided accurate directions in Episode 1. The claim borrows its credibility from those previous verified facts. This is the trap inside the claim: it is structurally identical in form to the kind of practical knowledge that has proven true before.

## The Topographical Claim: What the Wolf Will Assert

The scene's central dramatic event is the Wolf raising a ford ahead. The manner is practical concern — not alarmist, not dramatic. The language of a creature that knows this terrain and is doing Red the courtesy of passing on what it knows.

**The planted inconsistency that must be legible before the scene ends:**

The Wolf places a ford ahead on the path, past the second bend. It describes the near bank as having taken overnight runoff — looks solid, but the top inch gives when weighted, especially toward the left side where the bank flattens. It names the position: *past the second bend, where the path comes back left after the old growth has fully thinned.*

The inconsistency: Red crossed a ford before the second bend. The near bank was firm. The approach was ordinary. What is being presented as a future hazard is a fact behind her.

**The retrieval shape for Episode 3:**

*You said the ford was past the second bend. I crossed it before the second bend. The near bank was firm.* Three facts, one sentence. The scene must make all three elements legible before it ends — the Wolf's claimed position (past the second bend), Red's actual crossing (before the second bend), and the condition of the near bank at the actual crossing (firm, no give).

## The Mismatch: Sensation Before Name

The wrongness does not arrive as logic. The scene must not write it as a deduction.

The Wolf names the ford — *ford* or *crossing*, whichever word lands — and the word attempts to locate itself in Red's ongoing mental record of the walk she has taken this morning. It does not land. The word floats: understood in its general meaning but without a referent in front of her on the path. Her mental map of the walk has its ford, and it is behind her.

The floating is not a thought she has. It is a failure of fit.

**The physical register:**

Her gaze drops — not toward the Wolf, toward the ground ahead of her feet. This is her established habit from the performed scenes: she looks at the path surface when the terrain's accounting has not balanced (the gaze-drop pattern appears throughout Episode 1 in the terrain-reading sequences). Three steps of continued walking. The basket shifts slightly in her hand — not a check, just the ordinary redistribution of a weight carried in motion. During those three steps, the word *ford* moves against what her shoes already know: the near bank was firm, the crossing was before the second bend, the approach was ordinary.

The shape of the wrongness has a location before she has a name for it. The name arrives later, after the probe and the revision. What the scene dramatizes here is the floating: sensation preceding conclusion.

**The sequencing requirement:**

The scene fails this moment if it gives Red an interior thought in the form *she realized the ford the Wolf was describing was the one she had already crossed.* The realization is the conclusion. What precedes it is the failure of fit. Three steps must come first.

## The Probe: Form and Architecture

Red does not announce the discovery. The probe takes the form she has been using all episode: the lateral re-ask, the specific detail turned into a navigation question, the repeated word embedded in something that sounds like clarification rather than challenge.

**Required structural elements:**

The probe asks about the ford's position relative to a turning. It contains the word *crossing* or *ford* — the word that floated. It approaches the inconsistency from the angle of personal error in her own map: she might have the sequence wrong, she might be misremembering. It names the landmark the Wolf gave (*the second bend*) inside a question that treats the position as something she is asking help to confirm for herself.

The voice demonstration texture from her speech profile applies here — *Sorry, could you show me which turning? I thought — no, I might have it wrong.* Not that sentence; that texture. A fragment with a self-correction. The uncertainty voice. The form preserves her courtesy cover and commits the Wolf to a position it will have to hold or revise.

**What the probe must not do:**

Arrive before the three steps. The floating and the gaze-drop establish the body's processing time. The probe forms during those three steps and emerges as a question, not as a declaration Red has been preparing.

Not come too bright. She has not yet fully assembled what she knows. The uncertainty in the probe is partly performed and partly real — she is confirming the inconsistency by asking, not announcing it as already confirmed.

## The Wolf's Recovery: Required Properties and Cost

The Wolf's answer comes quickly. This is the tell.

**What the recovery must do:**

Move the landmark rather than correct the original claim. The ford was past the second bend; pressed on the position, the Wolf adjusts — the second bend, or just after where the canopy comes all the way down, or the place where the path comes back left — in a way that makes the original claim vanish rather than stand corrected. A true description of a path does not need its landmarks to move when questioned. The displacement is the second wrongness.

The revision is smooth enough that a less attentive listener would follow it without noting that the original position has been replaced rather than refined. An attending audience can trace the substitution. Red holds both: the original claim and the revised claim. The second claim has its own small wrongness.

**What the recovery costs the Wolf:**

It has spent a certainty of manner. The specific quality of authority that belonged to the original description — confident, specific, the language of a creature that has walked this path — required that description to go unchallenged. The quick recovery is a resource expenditure: slightly more compressed than the original description, then expanding again as the warmth reconstitutes. The compression is the tell. The warmth after it sounds like the warmth before it — that is what makes the revision convincing to a less attentive listener and what makes the second wrongness available only to an attending one.

The Wolf will calibrate subsequent approaches on the assumption that its expertise claim remains intact. That assumption is wrong.

## Red's Interior During and After the Exchange

**During the Wolf's recovery:** Red holds both the original claim and its displacement. She does not name either aloud. The holding is active — it requires the same quiet effort as every other piece of private inventory she has accumulated across Episode 1.

**After the probe and recovery:** In the four or five steps between the thank-you and the scene's close, the inconsistency is present in her inventory as a named set of facts rather than a sensation of wrongness. Not a full argument — she has not assembled *the Wolf invented a hazard, its description borrowed the authority of the flattery, both were parts of the same design* into a completed case. But she has three specific facts: the ford was before the second bend; the Wolf placed it after; the Wolf moved the landmark when she asked.

**The distinction that matters for Episode 3:** She is not retrieving a memory of unease. She is retrieving specific facts that have been filed since this scene. Three facts, one sentence, available at the threshold.

## The Scene's Close: Texture and Requirements

Red thanks the Wolf and resumes walking. The exchange closes on its surface as a question that found its answer.

**The thank-you's register:** Genuinely polite, in the same register as every previous thank-you in Episode 1 — *that helps, thank you* is the established texture from Scene 3 of Episode 1. Not warmer than those. Not colder. Nothing in its surface announces the filing beneath it. Her established speech profile is explicit: spoken courtesy can cover a private *absolutely not.* What remains private is not available to the other character.

**The resumed walking:** Forward, basket in her left hand. The bread is cool; grandmother is still at the path's other end. The close requires no backward glance, no tightened grip, no visible marker of what she is carrying. What it requires is the forward step — not the lightened walk of a person whose question found its answer, but the continued steady pace of a person carrying what she came in with plus something new. The distinction is fine enough that the Wolf cannot see it. An attending audience can, if the scene has placed both the original claim and the displacement in clear view before this moment.

**The structural irony's preservation:** The Wolf believes the recovery held. It reads the thank-you and the resumed walking as evidence that the revision landed. It does not know what she filed. This gap — between what the Wolf believes it has neutralized and what Red has entered into her private inventory — must be intact at the scene's close. The scene fails if it gives Red any exterior signal that announces the filing: a changed grip, a held breath, a pause before the thank-you that runs long enough to carry weight. The Wolf's misreading of that courtesy is the irony's engine.

## Immediate Pressures

**The errand's continuing claim.** The bread is cool. The clock is running. Grandmother is further down the path, still at the same destination. That purpose has not changed; the encounter has made it harder to hold in full attention. The effort of reading the Wolf's claim takes some of the attention she brought for the path.

**The credibility the previous accuracy established.** The Wolf's directions in Episode 1 proved correct. The observation that named her attentiveness was accurate. The claim about the softer ground past the old growth was accurate — she felt it arrive underfoot exactly as described. This makes the ford's description structurally credible on its surface; it is the same voice, the same register, the same idiom that has been verified three times over. The wrongness has to fight against that established track record. It wins — but the fighting is the scene's interior work.

**The probe's social cost.** Asking the question requires continuing a conversation that already sits in her inventory with three unresolved items. Each exchange is a small expenditure. She is further into this day's attention budget than she was at the gate. The probe is the right tool — she has been practicing this form all episode — but the accumulation is real and the budget is not unlimited.

**The accumulation's texture.** This is the fourth encounter. The three items in inventory from Episode 1 have not resolved. The management cost has been building since Scene 1 of Act 2 in Episode 1, when the offer of company first required the kind of social-accounting attention she did not bring for the Wolf. Each subsequent exchange has drawn from the same budget. The scene opens in that accumulated cost; the crack it produces will add a fourth item to inventory. This is the sharpest moment of vigilance fatigue so far — the active work of checking the Wolf's description against the terrain is more demanding than anything in the preceding scenes.

## Sensory Vocabulary

**The thank-you — ordinary form, ordinary timing:**
The same courtesy she has deployed at every previous exchange where she received something from the Wolf. *That helps, thank you* is the established texture. The thank-you in this scene must land in its ordinary shape and ordinary timing. Its ordinariness is the irony's protection.

## Open Character Questions for Phase 6

These are not unresolved contradictions. They are the decisions that belong to the writing phase and roundtable alone. Phase 6 establishes the ordered beat sequence; the roundtable gives them lived expression.

**1. The exchange before the claim.** The scene opens with the morning continuing, the old growth thinned, the path softer underfoot. Something has been said between them — the exchange needs enough ordinary texture to make the ford's introduction arrive as a practical continuation rather than a sudden subject change. The writing phase finds what the opening exchange is. It should carry the tone of continued walking-together practical conversation.

**2. The specific phrasing of the Wolf's claim.** The structural requirements are fixed: *past the second bend* must precede *the near bank takes the runoff*, so the position is on record before the hazard's detail arrives. The unhurried Southern cadence — clauses unfolding comfortably, the important condition saved for the end. The exact words are the writing phase's.

**3. The three steps and gaze-drop.** Their specific physical texture in the scene — the form of the basket's ordinary shift, the quality of the gaze-drop (toward what section of the path surface, from what distance), what the three steps look like in motion. Not announced, not named by Red, not explained to the audience. Expressed through physical staging.

**4. The probe's exact form.** A navigation question containing the word *ford* or *crossing*, naming *the second bend* or equivalent, arriving in the uncertainty voice demonstration texture. The fragment with the self-correction mid-sentence. The specific words are the writing phase's.

**5. The Wolf's recovery's compression and re-expansion.** The quickness — it comes before elaboration, not alongside it. The landmark moving rather than being corrected. The specific new position it is moved to, and the small wrongness of the new description. The brief compression of cadence followed by the warmth reconstituting. Exactly how this sounds is the writing phase's determination.

**6. The thank-you's arrival.** Its ordinary timing. What Red says in the moment after the recovery, in the space before the thank-you — does she say nothing, or something so brief it constitutes no announcement? The writing phase determines the specific form of the surface close.

**7. Red's resumed walking.** The specific texture of continued forward motion carrying weight alongside momentum. No performed signal; no tightened grip; the same pace she brought in. What this looks like in the final beats of the scene is the writing phase's staging decision.
