# Dramatic Action Brief — *The Offer of Company*
## Issue 69 · Season 1, Episode 1, Act 2, Scene 1
## Phase 7 — Yesod

---

### Overview

Nine sequential beats. Each beat depends on the one before it. The scene's drama lives in the calibration between beats — not the refusal itself, but the work Red does to shape it while protecting what she needs. The Wolf's conditional warmth makes its first public appearance. Red files the fact.

All three instruments — the basket, the directional orientation, and the Wolf's stillness — remain available across their assigned beats. None require choice between them. Character choices, dialogue, and specific lines remain open.

---

### Shared Facts at Scene Entry

- Red is on the left path past the long straight, in terrain that is becoming less self-explanatory. The canopy has closed back down; the elevated dry ridge of Scene 2 is behind her. The path's spine is ordinary: packed earth, occasional root-crossings, ground texture varying underfoot without announcing it.
- The basket is in her left hand, where it has been since the fork. The bread is warm-ish, not warm. The jar is cooler beneath.
- The Wolf was last seen at the dry edge (Scene 2, Beat 8). Its position on this new stretch is not yet visible to Red.
- Red carries two unresolved items from the prior scenes: (1) the birchwood word — the Wolf named the correct turning before she had identified her route — registered as a brief fractional tightening of the grip (Scene 2, Beat 6), filed without destination; (2) the unresolved sound from Scene 1, Beat 5 — environmental, not repeated, not named. Neither item has been spoken aloud.
- The quip-pattern (ironic private recovery from odd facts) has not operated twice in prior scenes. That pattern's absence carries forward.
- The Wolf has one learning from Scene 2: she can extract the useful part of an offer while declining its terms. Its adjustment for this scene is the bundled offer — company and direction packaged without a seam, so the separation costs her the useful content.
