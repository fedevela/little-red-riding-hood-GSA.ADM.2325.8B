The scene inherits late-day, post-rain woods; a narrowing trace beyond the
split alder; the pale stone, bark mark, and unequal edges now behind Red; the
retied fastening and intact covered basket; and Red's uninjured body. The
longer visible detour has already made the journey less efficient. The Wolf
follows at a readable distance and has observed that Red compares its claims
with physical evidence. The earlier beech, creek, branch, and route marks
remain behind them. Nothing establishes the Wolf's exact later position,
Red's destination details, or the safety of the route ahead.

## Dramatic through-line

The scene tests whether discernment can protect Red's purpose without freeing
her from the encounter. A genuine bodily difficulty in the wet trace gives the
Wolf a credible opening. Its offer must be materially useful, but the useful
thing becomes available only if Red supplies company, closeness, or continued
attention. Red can refuse that condition while retaining the basket and the
route. The Wolf then makes the cost of refusal felt through a small cooling of
welcome and a danger attached to an observable sign, not through a proven fact
or physical obstruction.

The decisive change is attentional. Red's authority survives: she keeps the
basket, chooses the next footing, and continues toward grandmother. But she
cannot move with the errand's earlier clean rhythm because she must compare the
Wolf's mood and warning with the ground. The audience should see the gain and
the loss at once: Red identifies the conditional pattern, yet the Wolf has made
monitoring itself part of the journey. No safe exit, capture, escape, rescue,
reconciliation, or final trap is established.

The scene's answer to the development question is a repeated, physically
observable return to the path. Red does not win an argument and does not need
to name the Wolf's motive. Each attempt to resume walking is interrupted by a
claim that requires one more look back. The cost culminates when she reaches a
new threshold still holding the basket, but with her attention divided.

## Character context and open authorship

### Red — `little_red_riding_hood`

- **Objective context:** Deliver provisions to grandmother and complete the
  journey through her own judgment; preserve the right to choose distance and
  company.
- **Pressure:** The ground is tiring, the light is thinning, and a practical
  offer may answer a real need. Refusal risks coldness or a warning; acceptance
  risks making closeness the price of help.
- **Subtext available to the character:** She may want recognition of her
  competence while refusing to let the Wolf define competence as obedience.
  She can treat the basket, the trace, and the next foothold as evidence more
  reliable than approval.
- **Tone conditions:** Concrete, courteous if possible, increasingly brief
  when a distinction must be protected. Any fear, pride, irritation, or
  private uncertainty remains hers to author.

### Wolf — `wolf`

- **Objective context:** Bring Red's attention and choices within its reach by
  making itself the reason the journey feels easier or safer.
- **Pressure:** Red's prior tests have limited its authority. It must preserve
  an appearance of concern while making refusal feel costly enough to reopen
  the exchange.
- **Subtext available to the character:** It may call closeness companionship
  and its own conditionality generosity. It can adapt from practical help to
  urgency when the first offer fails, but its reading of Red remains fallible.
- **Tone conditions:** Measured and attentive at first; warmth can become
  thinner, sharper, or more injured after refusal. Any impatience is a risk to
  its kindly appearance, not a required revelation of its motive.

## Ordered beat preparation

### `4db8a22db762:e1:b1` — practical relief with a price

**Assignment:** The Wolf offers to carry or guide Red through the worsening
trace only if she remains close and responsive, making relief depend on access
to her attention. **Pivotal identity:** none.

Give the Wolf a real observation to work from: the damp shelf shifts, the
basket makes Red's balance more deliberate, or the route tightens. The offer
should sound useful before its condition becomes audible. “Stay with me” may
be framed as the natural requirement of guidance, but the staging must leave a
clear alternative distance available. Red's body can test whether the burden
is manageable; she need not accept the Wolf's definition of what help means.

- Red's objective context: protect the provisions and continue without trading
  company for relief.
- Wolf's objective context: make attention feel like the ordinary payment for
  assistance and learn whether Red will close the distance.
- Sensory attention: wet root, basket weight, a held step, and the measurable
  gap between the two bodies.
- Dramatic change: a physical need becomes an interpersonal condition.

### `4db8a22db762:e1:b2` — bounded refusal and retained ownership

**Assignment:** Red refuses the condition while retaining the basket, tests the
burden and footing herself, and keeps the visible route toward grandmother
open. **Pivotal identity:** none.

The refusal need not be triumphant. It can be a plain boundary, a shortened
answer, or an action that declines the offered hand without insulting the
useful information. Red should make the route physically legible again by
checking her own footing and moving the basket securely. The audience must see
that refusal changes the terms of the encounter but does not remove Red from
the path or give the Wolf authority over it.

- Red's objective context: prove the next step to herself, not to the Wolf.
- Wolf's objective context: determine whether a softer appeal or a sharper
  consequence will produce the closeness it could not request directly.
- Sensory attention: sole against mud, cloth tightening over the basket,
  forward-facing shoulders, and the Wolf left outside her chosen distance.
- Dramatic change: Red preserves a boundary, but the Wolf receives refusal as
  information and a threat to its influence.

### `4db8a22db762:e1:b3` — changed welcome and unverifiable danger

**Assignment:** The Wolf cools its welcome and attaches an increasingly urgent
danger to Red's departure, offering claims partly observable but not verifiable
from her position. **Pivotal identity:** none.

Let the first change be small enough that Red must decide whether she noticed
it: the Wolf stops offering the easy tone, lets a silence lengthen, or recasts
her refusal as needless difficulty. It can point to a real sign—water moving
under the bank, a fresh break, a darkening section of path—then enlarge that
sign into a warning about what will happen if she continues alone. The claim
must remain a claim. The Wolf can make departure socially costly without
standing in the way: concern becomes reproach, welcome becomes conditional,
and urgency asks Red to spend another answer.

- Red's objective context: separate evidence from pressure while keeping the
  route and basket under her control.
- Wolf's objective context: turn its changed treatment into a reason Red should
  reopen the failed offer.
- Sensory attention: the empty interval after Red's refusal, a visible but
  ambiguous woodland sign, and the Wolf's voice occupying the space behind her.
- Dramatic change: refusal now has a felt cost, and Red's departure requires
  monitoring rather than simple forward motion.

### `4db8a22db762:e1:b4` — measured onward movement under surveillance

- Red's objective context: keep moving toward grandmother while deciding how

- Wolf's objective context: make its uncertainty impossible for Red to ignore

- Sensory attention: shortened footfalls, basket shift, wet ground answering

- Dramatic change: discernment becomes costly labor; the path remains open but
