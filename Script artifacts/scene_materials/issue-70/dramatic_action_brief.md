# Dramatic Action Brief — *You Notice Things*
## Issue 70 · Season 1, Episode 1, Act 2, Scene 2
## Phase 7 — Yesod

---

### Overview

Nine sequential beats. This scene closes Act 2. Its mechanism — accurate observation as lever for a presumptuous extension — operates across four structural beats (B004 through B007), bracketed by the arrival beat (B001–B003) and the closing inventory (B008–B009). The drama does not live in confrontation; it lives in the quality of what is named, what is placed inside that naming, and what Red carries forward without putting down.

The beats are dependent in order. Each beats rests on the state the previous one established. Character choices, dialogue, and specific lines remain open.

---

### Shared Facts at Scene Entry

- Red has navigated the birchwood fork using the Wolf's Scene 3 direction. The direction was accurate. The fork is behind her. She is on the path to the far houses.
- The basket is in her left hand. It has been there since the errand-hand reassignment at the close of Scene 3 (Beat 9 of issue-69). The bread is past warm-ish — cooler now, not cold. The jar beneath the cloth remains heavier and cool. The warmth is past its useful range.
- The wet right shoe: present and background since Act 1, Scene 1, Beat 3. Neither resolved nor mentioned since.
- Red carries two unresolved private items: (1) the birchwood inconsistency — the Wolf named the correct turning for the far houses before Red had identified her route, registered as a fractional grip-tightening (Scene 2, Beat 6), never voiced, retaining the texture of something testable; (2) the endearment-and-cooling sequence from Scene 3 — the *honey* mid-offer, the warmth that returned after her refusal with fewer clauses and the endearment absent, registered as a rhythm change, not yet convertible to a statement. Neither has been spoken aloud.
- The Wolf's Scene 3 direction proved accurate at every point (roots for twenty paces, bend left, split birch, white bark). Its practical credibility enters this scene unbroken.
- The Wolf enters having revised. Scene 3 confirmed that Red can separate an offer's useful part from its terms. The revision is not a new strategy; it is a different instrument: less transactional, more observational. It has been watching for what to name. Its rebuilt warmth carries, barely audibly, the trace of the Scene 3 cooling. That trace is what makes this warmth more precisely aimed.
- The terrain past the birchwood fork: old-growth trees, trunks with presence, roots spreading into the path surface. A section ahead where several large roots from different trees cross the path's spine in close succession, their relative heights ambiguous from a few steps away. This section requires active reading before weight is committed. The flat autumn morning light gives the crossings their clearest surface definition from the approach angle.
- Errand temporal pressure: the bread has been cooling for the full length of the episode. Grandmother's destination has not moved. The clock is running at an ordinary rate.
