## Dramatic through-line

Red's withheld destination is not a puzzle she owes the Wolf. She knows where
she is going and enough about the route to compare spoken certainty with what
the ground permits. The scene gives her a repeatable method: establish a
private reference point, ask for concrete landmarks, inspect both available
edges, and prefer the visible option when the Wolf's urgency is not supported
by the ground. Her knowledge grows, but the procedure costs her time, route
distance, and some privacy.

The Wolf is accurate about the washout's difficulty and fallible about its
necessity. It can see Red count, mark, withhold, and verify. It may adapt to
those observations, but it cannot know her private destination or read her
mind. Its offer of help should have practical plausibility while making
proximity to Red or the basket part of the implied price. Red's refusal of
that price does not make either edge safe and does not resolve the danger.

The audience should feel a genuine gain in discernment alongside a narrowing
of available time. The pivotal movement is not “Red proves the Wolf false and
escapes”; it is “Red identifies an overstated claim, chooses the longer line,
and leaves the Wolf better equipped to pursue her.”

## Sensory and formal vocabulary

### `<SCENE_HEADING>`

Place the scene beyond the flat stones, on a wet trace narrowing toward darker
trees. The split alder has broken across a shallow washout: its roots hold one
side in a tense shelf, while its crown and torn bank make the obstruction
legible without making passage impossible. The creek markers and original
beech are absent from the immediate playing space.

### `<TRANSITION>`

Begin with the established forward movement and the Wolf's readable distance.
Let the uninterrupted walk become a stop at the pale stone, then a count, a
small mark, a question, and a comparison of edges. Red's route resumes only
after the test, and the longer detour should be felt as a real loss of ground.
End with the detour active and the Wolf having observed the method, without
choosing capture, escape, rescue, reconciliation, or a final threshold.

### `<CAMERA>`

Use a medium tracking view that keeps Red's hood, covered basket, feet, and
the continuing trace legible. Widen to preserve the Wolf's distance and the
two edges of the washout in one field. Move close only for the pale-stone
count, the route mark, hand/foot tests, and the basket fastening; return to a
balanced two-shot whenever the Wolf's offer tries to convert information into
closeness. The decisive image is the longer visible detour beside the Wolf's
confident claim, not a reaction shot declaring who is safe.

### `<LIGHTING>`

Cool, thinning late-day light comes through wet canopy. Silver on alder bark,
mud, and the pale stone makes the ground inspectable. The washout's shaded
edge is less readable than its exposed edge, but neither is lit as safe. Each
pause lets the remaining light diminish slightly. The visual cost of the test
is that the route can still be chosen while the day gives Red less time to
choose it.

### `<AUDIO>`

Carry forward wet leaf litter, drops, soft bracken, distant residual creek,
and the basket's contained movement. Establish the split alder through a
small bank collapse, root creak, or water undercut rather than an oversized
effect. The pale-stone count and route mark should interrupt the walking rhythm
audibly. The Wolf's voice remains measured; Red's practical checks create
short silences in which the ground, not the Wolf, answers. The detour should
end with longer footfalls and the possibility of the Wolf following, not with a
musical resolution.

### `<ACTION>`

Keep Red's free hand available and the basket in her possession. The physical
sequence may include: fixing the pale stone as a reference; making a small
private mark in damp bark, mud, or leaf litter; asking what visible landmark
lies beyond the alder; testing the nearer and farther edges without crossing
into the Wolf's space; looking back far enough to compare the obstruction
with the mark; and taking the longer line. The mark must be ordinary and
recoverable, not a device that guarantees return.

The Wolf can indicate the preferred edge, point out a real weak place, change
its posture, or offer to come nearer. It must not touch Red, take the basket,
block either edge, compel a direction, or establish its invented danger as
fact.

### `<PARENTHETICAL>`

Physical directions should make observation available without prescribing
private feeling. For Red, specify the basket, the pale stone, the route mark,
footing, the two edges, and the maintained distance. For the Wolf, specify its
readable position, gaze, indicated ground, and any small adjustment that
reveals it has learned what Red is doing. Leave whether Red feels pride,
fear, irritation, or renewed confidence for her authorship.

### `<DIALOGUE>`

The language should distinguish visible fact from claimed necessity. Red may
withhold the exact destination and remaining distance while asking for
landmarks that can be checked: what stands beyond the alder, where the trace
rejoins firm ground, and what makes one edge preferable. The Wolf may answer
partly accurately, then turn preference into necessity and offer help whose
benefit depends on proximity. Red can accept a fact without accepting the
Wolf's authority. No source labels, institutional language, magical knowledge,
or authorial lesson belongs in the speech.

## Ordered beat preparation

### `ab633ed98ca8:e1:b1` — withheld destination, visible continuity

**Assignment:** Red enters beyond the flat stones with the fastening retied,
keeps grandmother's exact location undisclosed, and compares the Wolf's
account with the visible trace.

**Preparation:** Preserve forward walking as the baseline. Let the basket's
secure fastening and the uninterrupted trace give Red concrete evidence of
what is true now. The Wolf can ask, infer, or describe the route, but Red's
answer need not confirm the destination or remaining distance. The audience
should see that withholding is an active route-protection choice, not a loss of
purpose.

- Red's objective context: continue the errand with provisions intact and
  decide what information is useful enough to answer.
- Wolf's objective context: turn observed direction and distance into access to
  Red's plan; its inference remains fallible.
- Sensory attention: retied fastening, wet trace, fading light, the gap between
  the two bodies, and the absence of earlier markers.
- Change carried forward: Red's earlier tests have become a habit of checking,
  while the Wolf has learned that practical accuracy earns only provisional
  trust.

### `ab633ed98ca8:e1:b2` — the split alder as unequal obstruction

**Assignment:** A split alder has washed part of the bank away, leaving two
physically possible edges with unequal footing, exposure, and delay.

**Preparation:** Make the obstruction passable but consequential. One edge may
be exposed and firmer yet longer; the other may be shorter but slumped,
root-crossed, and close to the undercut bank. Neither edge should be staged as
the obvious safe answer. The audience needs enough geography to judge the
Wolf's later certainty against the same ground Red can inspect.

- Red's objective context: learn what the obstruction actually permits before
  accepting advice.
- Wolf's objective context: use superior terrain familiarity to make its
  preferred edge seem like the only responsible choice.
- Sensory attention: split trunk, pale exposed roots, wet clay, a low run of
  water, and the extra steps visible around the longer side.
- Dramatic function: a genuine physical problem gives the Wolf a legitimate
  opening, while unequal options preserve Red's agency.

### `ab633ed98ca8:e1:b3` — count, mark, and concrete questions

**Assignment:** Red counts from a pale stone, places a small route mark, and
asks for concrete landmarks beyond the obstruction without surrendering her
destination.

**Preparation:** The count is private enough to protect her exact route
knowledge but visible enough for the Wolf to notice. The mark should connect
this place to the trace she has just walked, allowing a comparison rather than
promising a safe retreat. Questions should demand checkable landmarks, not
confession: what lies past the alder, where the trace becomes firm, and how the
Wolf knows. The procedure should slow her and make the cost of discernment
physical.

- Red's objective context: create a method she can repeat when spoken certainty
  exceeds visible evidence.
- Wolf's objective context: understand the rule Red is using and whether its
  information can be shaped to fit that rule.
- Sensory attention: fingertip or shoe against pale stone, a small mark in wet
  material, counted steps, the basket held close, and the Wolf watching rather
  than approaching.
- Open authorship: the exact number, mark, and wording remain available to Red
  and should not be forced by preparation.

### `ab633ed98ca8:e1:b4` — necessity claim and conditional help

**Assignment:** The Wolf turns the washout into a claim that its preferred edge
is necessary and offers practical help whose usefulness would bring it closer
to Red and the basket.

**Preparation:** The Wolf's account should contain a true observation—one
edge is materially worse in some respect—before it overreaches into “must” or
“only.” Its offer may involve pointing from nearer ground, steadying the
 basket, or showing the foothold, but the staging must make proximity the
unspoken price. Red has already learned that useful information need not grant
the Wolf touch or company; the scene lets her apply that learning under more
pressure.

- Red's objective context: separate useful warning from manufactured urgency
  while keeping the basket and distance hers.
- Wolf's objective context: make its preferred route and its presence appear
  necessary, preserving the appearance that Red chooses closeness.
- Sensory attention: the Wolf's indicated edge, the actual alternate footing,
  the open space between bodies, and the light lost while the offer hangs.
- Emotional pressure: a refusal can risk the Wolf's warmth or invite a cooler
  judgment, but no reaction is predetermined.
