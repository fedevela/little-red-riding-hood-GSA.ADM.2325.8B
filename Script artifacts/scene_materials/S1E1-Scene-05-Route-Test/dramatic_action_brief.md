# Dramatic Action Brief — The Split Alder Test

## Scene frame

`S1E1-Scene-05-Route-Test` is one continuous scene in Act 1, beyond the flat
stones on the narrowing wet trace. The creek markers, the original fallen-beech
fork, and the earlier crossing remain behind Red and are not replayed. Red
enters with the intact basket and its fastening retied. The Wolf follows at a
distance. Late-day light is thinning after rain.

The fresh split-alder washout is a local, inspectable obstruction: two edges
remain physically possible, but they differ in footing, exposure, and delay.
The Wolf may know useful terrain facts and may overstate what those facts
require. Its help can be offered in a way that seeks proximity to Red or the
basket, but it cannot touch, block, take, compel, or establish an invented
danger as fact. Red can use information without granting authority. Her route
test should produce a durable distinction between “worse” and “necessary,”
while costing time, distance, and some privacy about how she decides.

No dialogue, private thought, exact step count, exact mark, or discretionary
reaction is fixed here. The two performing characters are the only participants;
there are no non-speaking characters in this scene. Mother and grandmother
remain offstage and are present only through Red’s purpose and withheld
information. Capture, escape, rescue, reconciliation, safety, and the final
threshold remain unconfirmed.

## Action through-line

Red’s practical sequence is available as an authored choice: continue while
protecting the destination, stop when the split alder interrupts the line,
establish a private reference at the pale stone, make an ordinary route mark,
ask for concrete landmarks, inspect both edges, compare the Wolf’s necessity
claim with visible continuity, and choose the longer visible detour if the
ground does not support the claim. The mark is recoverable but not magical and
does not guarantee a safe return.

The Wolf’s pursuit remains adaptive but fallible. It can notice the count, mark,
withheld answers, repeated checks, and the detour. It cannot know Red’s private
destination or motive except through what she says and what its position lets it
observe. Its accurate description of a weak edge may coexist with a false or
overstated claim that the edge is the only possible passage. After Red’s test,
it may try to preserve influence by recasting the longer line as delay or
imprudence, but the scene must leave its next tactic unresolved.
