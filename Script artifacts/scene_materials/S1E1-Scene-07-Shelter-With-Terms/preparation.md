## Dramatic through-line

The scene turns the Wolf's earlier unverifiable warnings into a narrowing
choice. First it names something behind Red that she cannot independently see:
perhaps movement in the wet woods or a pursuit made urgent by the failing
light. When Red asks what it can actually know, the Wolf revises rather than
withdraws: the immediate danger becomes the gully's unstable ground. That
revision is credible enough to matter and inconsistent enough to expose its
method.

The two apparent options are the exposed shelf and the root-hollow. They are
not equal in practical arrangement. The shelf is visible, narrow, wet, and
costly to cross; the hollow is low, covered, and easier to enter because the
Wolf occupies the ground beside its mouth and indicates the route. The hollow
has a readable exit, so it is not an established prison. Its danger is that
the Wolf's presence and supervision make protection seem to require proximity.

The basket supplies the scene's physical hinge. Its fastening catches at the
hollow's roots or a splintered threshold. The Wolf can genuinely secure it,
but its offer should add a social condition: Red must let it come close, hand
over the basket, or remain beside it while it does the work. Red can keep her
possession and state that distance remains hers. The Wolf's response should
make refusal consequential without physically enforcing it: warmth thins,
the exposed shelf is described as less forgiving, and the hollow becomes the
“safest remaining” choice.

The pivotal change is recognition, not resolution. Red can map the physical
choice and identify the conditional structure, yet cannot measure what either
compliance or refusal will cost before the light and route narrow further. End
with her at the threshold, a measured response available but unperformed.
