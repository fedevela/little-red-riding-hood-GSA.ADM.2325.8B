# Yesod Dramatic-Action Brief — The Shelter with Terms

## Scene frame

This is one bounded Act 1 threshold scene at a washed-out gully in thinning
late-day light after rain. Red arrives from Scene 6 with the intact covered
basket, one hand free, an uninjured body, and the errand to grandmother still
active. The trace divides between an exposed narrow shelf above slick clay and
dark water and a low root-hollow with a visible but constricted rear opening.
The Wolf is readable beside or behind the hollow, close enough to make that
route easier without touching, blocking, redirecting, or compelling Red.

The action prepares a credible narrowing rather than a solved trap. The Wolf's
first danger claim is unverifiable and must contract when Red tests it. A real
footing problem and a real basket snag make shelter and assistance useful; the
Wolf's position and terms make that usefulness predatory. Red can inspect,
retain the basket, state a boundary, or pause, but no response is prescribed.
The scene ends before entry, escape, capture, rescue, reconciliation, or safe
exit. Mother and grandmother remain offstage presences carried by Red's
purpose; no non-speaking participant acts in this scene.

## Authorship and observation boundaries

These notes establish stimuli, available knowledge, stakes, and playable
affordances, not exact dialogue, private thoughts, gestures, or decisions.
Red can judge visible ground, the hollow's opening and exit, the basket, the
Wolf's distance, and changes in its manner. She cannot verify the claimed
danger beyond what she can inspect. The Wolf can observe Red's checks, pauses,
answers, basket, and chosen distance; it cannot know her private calculation,
her full destination, or what account she may carry home. Its predatory purpose
remains concealed intention, not knowledge available to Red or a fact that the
terrain proves.

## Continuity carried forward

The prior scenes establish the late-day post-rain progression, the trace and
its marks behind Red, the intact basket and retied fastening, Red's lack of
injury, and the Wolf's non-obstructive following. The immediate inheritance
from Scene 6 is the pale-stone pause, a visible trace ahead, and the Wolf at a
readable distance. The gully, root-hollow, exposed shelf, snag, and changing
light are new local conditions compatible with that progression. No earlier
knowledge is reset, and no final outcome is introduced.

## Dramatic progression

The scene should move from a claim to a test, from a test to a physical
comparison, and from comparison to a choice whose terms are not equal. The
hollow must be genuinely useful enough that hesitation is rational. The Wolf
can revise, wait, point, offer strength, or cool its warmth, but its body must
remain guidance and influence rather than a physical barrier. The final image
holds Red with the basket at the threshold, both route lines visible, and the
Wolf controlling the convenient ground while the next response remains hers.
