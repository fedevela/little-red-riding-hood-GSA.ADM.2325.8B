- **Scene title:** Welcome With Terms
- **Placement:** after the marked creek crossing, on the continuing rain-marked trace
- **Readiness:** ready-scene assignment received
- **Accepted scene anchor:** `8a9a7a25ceee:3:a1`
- **Anchor content:** The divergent proposals resolve into one escalating encounter: a measured pause establishes that the Wolf can honor distance, a practical crossing makes its knowledge briefly affirming, and a later request for closeness converts that usefulness into conditional warmth; keeping these movements in one scene preserves the Wolf’s plausible deniability while making Red’s boundary and the journey’s cost cumulative.

This preparation carries the assignment into sensory and emotional conditions. It
does not write final script prose or determine either character’s private
response. Red and the Wolf own their thoughts, speech, and actions within the
opportunities and constraints below.

## Continuity entering the scene

The immediately preceding performed scene is `crossing-conditional-help` in
`Script/Season_01/Episode_01/script.md`. Its continuity notes establish:

- Red has crossed the rain-swollen creek and continues along the narrowing trace
  with the covered basket, provisions, destination, and judgment intact.
- The three short marks and the angled branch remain at the far bank behind her.
  The earlier two-twig mark and fallen tree remain farther back toward the fork.
- The late-day light is thinning after rain. No injury, loss of provisions, or
  unsafe crossing has been established.
- The Wolf previously gave accurate, bounded guidance without carrying the
  basket, placing Red’s feet, blocking either bank, or receiving authority over
  her route. Its reading of that cooperation remains fallible.

The scene therefore begins after a practical success, not at a new meeting. A
pause can briefly let the audience feel the relief of shared quiet and the
possibility that the Wolf’s distance is a chosen courtesy. The later crossing
and bargain must grow from that relief, while Red’s errand remains the physical
line that the Wolf’s attention begins to burden.

## Dramatic continuity and development decision

### Threads entering

- **Red’s discernment — red / #B33A3A:** She has learned that the Wolf can be
  right about a patch of ground. This scene asks her to separate accurate help,
  gratitude, and cooperation from permission for company or closeness.
- **The Wolf’s enclosure — moss / #52634A:** Its earlier guidance won only a
  bounded opening. It now tries to turn the appearance of welcome into a claim,
  while preserving the story that Red can refuse.
- **The unanswered threshold — amber / #B98532:** Red can continue physically,
  but the path becomes harder to inhabit when she must monitor the Wolf’s mood.
  The scene foreshadows narrowing without resolving capture, escape, rescue, or
  the final trap.

### Exchange that makes warmth conditional

The clearest exchange is a practical offer after the crossing: the Wolf can
show the easiest footing or remain close enough to point out the next turns,
but says this is worthwhile only if Red stays beside it or within reach. Red
can acknowledge that the previous guidance helped while refusing the added
closeness. The Wolf can answer that nothing is preventing her from going on;
it is simply no longer inclined to spend its attention on someone who does not
want its company. This preserves plausible deniability while making the price
felt: the path stays open, but ease, information, and warmth are selectively
withheld.

The offer must remain genuinely optional in material terms. The Wolf does not
block the trace, seize the basket, threaten immediate harm, or invent a danger
that only it can physically prevent. Its changed treatment is the pressure.

## Sensory and performance vocabulary

### `<SCENE_HEADING>`

`ACT 1 — SCENE: WELCOME WITH TERMS`, a rain-darkened pause and a difficult
stretch of trace beyond the creek, late day after rain. The playing space should
retain a readable direction back toward the creek and a visible continuation
forward. Red’s basket and the Wolf’s several-pace distance are essential
geography.

### `<TRANSITION>`

Carry the measured footfalls from the crossing into a pause where Red checks
the basket and route. Let quiet last long enough for distance to feel like a
form of respect. A renewed obstacle then interrupts the pause: slick roots,
soft ground, or a narrow shelf that can be crossed by testing. The final
transition is Red’s onward movement with no physical barrier, but with the
Wolf’s altered warmth following as an unseen weight in the rhythm.

### `<CAMERA>`

If filmed, open in a wide two-shot that makes the Wolf’s several paces of
distance legible and keeps Red’s basket, the route behind, and the route ahead
in one field. Settle briefly on Red’s hands checking the cover and on the Wolf
waiting without approaching. During the crossing, follow Red’s foot and the
ground she tests; include the Wolf’s indication without making its body the
source of certainty. At the bargain, hold both faces and the space between
them. After Red names the boundary, let the frame retain the open path while
showing the Wolf’s changed position, still technically non-blocking.

### `<LIGHTING>`

Use cool, broken late-day light under wet leaves. The pause may contain one
small pocket of softer reflected light, allowing the attention at a distance to
feel briefly affirming without becoming magical or safe. As the bargain forms,
let that pocket narrow through ordinary canopy movement or dimming daylight.
The Wolf remains visible in readable shade; no lighting change should declare
its private intention. The open route ahead should remain visible enough that
Red’s freedom to proceed is undeniable.

### `<AUDIO>`

Begin with creek-water faintly behind them, residual drops, wet leaves, and the
basket’s small shift as Red settles it. The pause needs a genuine interval in
which shared quiet—not silence imposed by force—can be heard. Slick roots,
cloth, and careful shoes make the crossing tactile. The Wolf’s voice can first
arrive low and even, with space around Red’s replies; after the boundary, leave
more air between its words and replace warmth with clipped stillness or the
sound of resumed footfalls. The woods never announce that danger has become
certain.

### `<ACTION>`

Red checks the basket, studies the next ground, and chooses whether to accept
the Wolf’s indication. She may cross with her stick, one hand on a root, or by
testing each patch herself. The Wolf can remain at its chosen distance, point
out firmer footing, and later choose not to provide additional guidance. It may
take one step nearer only as a clearly observable offer that Red can decline;
it must not crowd, touch, pursue into her path, or make its body the obstacle.
After the boundary, the Wolf’s reserve should be enacted through distance,
withheld pointing, delayed answers, or a turned head—not through open force.

### `<PARENTHETICAL>`

Keep parentheticals playable: *(checking the basket)*, *(after the quiet)*,
*(testing the root)*, *(from several paces away)*, *(indicating the firmer
ground)*, *(nearer, but not touching)*, *(after a pause)*, *(turning toward the
path)*. Do not prescribe private feelings or call the Wolf’s treatment coercive
as an inner fact. Observable warmth, timing, distance, and withdrawal carry
the change.

### `<DIALOGUE>`

Red’s language is concrete and courteous at first: basket, mud, footing,
distance, road, creek, and grandmother. Her thanks can be specific without
becoming a promise. When the bargain is named, her speech should distinguish
“that helped” from “come closer,” and “I can continue” from “I welcome this
company.” A boundary may be plain without requiring her to explain the whole
danger.

The Wolf can call its closeness ordinary company, easier walking, or a fair
exchange. It can say Red is free to refuse while making clear that its favor
will not remain the same. Its wording should preserve its self-excusing claim
that it is offering a choice, while its changed warmth exposes the condition.
Neither character speaks an authorial lesson or knows the other’s private
thoughts.

## Ordered beat preparation

### 1. `8a9a7a25ceee:e1:b1` — distance as temporary welcome

- **Source content:** A pause or shared quiet gives Red a temporary sense that
  the Wolf can remain at a chosen distance.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red’s basket and breathing, then the measured space
  between the two bodies; let the quiet be received before it is interpreted.
- **Conditions:** The creek and its marks are behind them. The trace is wet but
  passable, the light is fading, and Red has a reason to pause and check her
  provisions or route.
- **Red context:** Objective: continue to grandmother with care intact. The
  scene offers a real chance to notice that the Wolf is not approaching and to
  accept the relief of that fact without deciding it means trust.
- **Wolf context:** It can choose stillness and attentive listening. Its
  intention and interpretation remain private and fallible; no inner motive is
  supplied by this preparation.
- **Dramatic function:** Establishes the brief affirmative experience that makes
  the later condition emotionally legible. Distance is felt as something the
  Wolf can grant or honor, not yet as a problem Red must solve.

### 2. `8a9a7a25ceee:e1:b2` — practical guidance during difficulty

- **Source content:** A damaged or slippery stretch interrupts Red’s progress;
  the Wolf identifies firmer footing or a sequence that can be tested.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red’s testing hand or shoe, the basket kept under her
  control, and the Wolf’s advice delivered across the existing distance.
- **Conditions:** The Wolf’s information is useful for this immediate patch but
  cannot guarantee the route beyond it. The ground remains ordinary, wet, and
  fallible.
- **Red context:** She may ask for a precise indication, compare it with what
  she sees, and accept only the information. Cooperation does not transfer her
  pace, basket, route, or distance to the Wolf.
- **Wolf context:** It has a concrete opportunity to make itself useful again.
  It may notice Red’s attention and mistake practical listening for personal
  welcome, but that mistake must remain its own inference.
- **Dramatic function:** Gives the Wolf a credible basis for the later bargain:
  its knowledge has helped twice, while keeping Red’s successful movement hers.

### 3. `8a9a7a25ceee:e1:b3` — boundary under conditional warmth

- **Source content:** Red accepts limited help while maintaining her chosen
  distance, then names the boundary when the Wolf makes continued warmth depend
  on closer company; she remains materially free to proceed, but refusal gains
  a social cost.
- **Pivotal identity:** **`BEAT-G4-3-1`**
- **Predecessor links:** `BEAT-4d6a1d8707fe-1-3`, `BEAT-B3-2B-1`,
  `BEAT-B4-2B-1`, `BEAT-B4-2B-2`, `BEAT-B4-2B-3`, `BEAT-B4-2B-4`,
  `BEAT-B4-2B-5`, `BEAT-C1-2C-1`, `BEAT-C1-2C-2`, `BEAT-CH2-2A-2`,
  `BEAT-CH3-2A-1`, `BEAT-CH3-2A-2`, `BEAT-CH3-2A-3`, `BEAT-CH3-2A-4`,
  `BEAT-CH3-2A-5`, `BEAT-G3-3-1`, `BEAT-K3-1-3`.
- **Audience attention:** The exact distinction Red makes, followed at once
  by the Wolf’s altered warmth. Keep the open path in view so the audience can
  see why the pressure is social and relational rather than physical force.
- **Conditions:** The Wolf can frame closeness as optional—walk beside me,
  remain within reach, or accept less of my attention. It cannot make the
  crossing impossible or claim that Red has agreed to company merely because
  she used its guidance.
- **Red context:** Her objective is to preserve the errand and her authority
  over company. Gratitude, politeness, and a partial welcome can coexist with a
  clear refusal. Her tone and exact words remain hers; the preparation supplies
  the contradiction she must answer.
- **Wolf context:** Its objective is to make useful attention a condition of
  easier movement while telling itself Red is freely choosing. The changed
  treatment may be formally calm, selectively withholding, or wounded in
  manner; its self-justification must not erase its responsibility.
- **Dramatic function:** Converts warmth into a price. This is the scene’s
  pivotal relational change: Red names the distance she needs, and the Wolf’s
  response teaches her that refusal can alter the world of the journey even
  when no one lays a hand on her.

### 4. `8a9a7a25ceee:e1:b4` — technically voluntary reserve

- **Source content:** The Wolf answers Red’s boundary with nonthreatening
  reserve and withholds some ease or guidance, making refusal costly without
  blocking the path.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** The difference between the unchanged geography and
  the changed social atmosphere: the same trace remains open, but the Wolf no
  longer offers itself as an easy companion.
- **Conditions:** The Wolf can decline to point out the next footing, answer
  only what is directly asked, or step back into shade. It must leave Red a
  visible route and enough material ability to continue.
- **Red context:** She can continue, ask once more, decide not to pursue the
  Wolf’s approval, or spend attention watching its mood. The scene does not
  decide whether she feels fear, anger, relief, or doubt.
- **Wolf context:** It can preserve the claim that it is merely respecting her
  choice while making its favor conditional in practice. Its reserve is an
  action, not proof that Red caused or deserved the change.
- **Dramatic function:** Makes the price immediate and legible without
  prematurely escalating to open threat. Red’s boundary changes the Wolf’s
  treatment and the atmosphere of the journey.

### 5. `8a9a7a25ceee:e1:b5` — onward with divided attention

- **Source content:** Red resumes the journey with provisions and destination
  intact, while monitoring the Wolf’s altered mood competes with her errand.
- **Pivotal identity:** none.
- **Audience attention:** Red’s forward route and covered basket first; then the
  glance, pause, or listening that shows the Wolf has occupied part of the
  attention meant for grandmother.
- **Conditions:** The path remains physically open. The Wolf has not captured,
  rescued, reconciled with, or safely released Red; the final trap remains
  ahead and unresolved.
- **Red context:** Her purpose survives the encounter, but not untouched. She
  may carry a more precise distinction between help and permission, alongside
  the burden of deciding whether to keep watching the Wolf.
- **Wolf context:** Its favor has been refused in the form it wanted, but the
  altered atmosphere may still serve its pursuit. It does not yet know whether
  reserve, renewed charm, or another tactic will work.
- **Dramatic function:** Carries the relational turn into the next scene. The
  audience should feel both Red’s continued agency and the cost of having to
  manage another creature’s response to her boundary.
- **Exit condition:** Red moves toward grandmother with the basket and route
  intact. The Wolf’s distance and withdrawn ease remain active pressure; no
  outcome is settled.

## Handoff responsibilities

Later scene-writing, performance, and revision phases must preserve the ordered
local beat references, scene identity, episode ownership, and pivotal identity
`BEAT-G4-3-1` with every predecessor link listed above. They should carry
forward the post-rain trace, Red’s covered basket, the creek marks and angled
branch behind her, the Wolf’s proven but bounded knowledge, the open path, and
the newly established cost attached to its warmth.

The performed scene must not establish mind-reading, magical compulsion,
transformation, a device that settles the conflict, open force, or a resolved
outcome. It must preserve Red’s dignity and the Wolf’s responsibility. The
audience should leave with the distinction between receiving kindness and
consenting to preferred company still alive in Red’s action, not explained by
an authorial voice.
