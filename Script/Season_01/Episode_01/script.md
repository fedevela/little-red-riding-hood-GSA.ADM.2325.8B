# Before the Clearing

---

# DRAMATIS PERSONAE

**Red** — A girl carrying provisions to her grandmother's house, at the threshold of independence.

**The Wolf** — A creature who knows the woods and approaches with concealed intent beneath an appearance of concern.

---

# SETTING & TIME

**Place:** A woodland path between a garden gate and grandmother's house, passing through birch stands and older trees.

**Time:** Early autumn morning.

---

### SCENE 1 — RED SETS OUT

> *(The garden gate has just caught behind her, its latch settling with the particular sound it always makes. The open stretch lies ahead — low autumn sun working sideways across the path, the ground still holding its early chill. The birch stand waits at the tree-line. The basket is in her left hand. Through the wrapping she can already feel the bread's warmth, and beneath it the distinct cooler weight of the jar. The morning is early and clean. She is moving well.)*

> *(RED: She walks out from the gate's shadow onto the open stretch, the low sun hitting the path at a sideways angle that makes the frost-grass glow. The basket swings once in her left hand and settles.)*

> *(RED: She adjusts her grip on the basket handle and tips it just slightly, feeling the bread's weight shift and the cooler, denser resistance of the jar underneath — both exactly where they should be.)*

> *(RED: She lets the cloth fall back over the basket's contents and straightens up, resuming her pace. The birch stand at the tree-line catches the early light, the white trunks going almost gold at the base.)*

> *(RED: She passes the fallen beech — right where it always is, on the left just before the path goes soft — and shifts her weight to the right instinctively, keeping her footing dry.)*

> *(RED: She enters the birch stand and the light changes — shade-and-stripe, cooler underfoot, the path narrowing just slightly. She doesn't slow down.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-67-B002] -->

> *(Inside the birch stand the light comes in vertical strips — bright bars alternating with filtered grey-green shadow. The path underfoot has shifted from packed earth to something softer, the roots coming up in configurations she has navigated enough times that her feet find the line before her attention does. The trunks are white and close. The morning birdsong continues, wrapped now in a slightly different acoustic. The older trees begin at the far edge of the stand; their light will be heavier when she reaches them.)*

> *(RED: She moves through the birch stand at a steady pace, her feet finding the familiar root-lines without her having to look down. The light comes in alternating bars — bright, shadow, bright — and she passes through them without breaking stride.)*

> *(RED: Her attention catches on one particular root — thick and arched, the bark gone silver-smooth where years of feet have crossed it, catching a slant of light at this specific angle so it looks almost luminous. She steps over it without breaking pace.)*

> *(RED: She transfers the basket from her left hand to her right, shaking out her left fingers briefly before letting it hang at her side again. The weight redistributes. She doesn't slow down.)*

> *(RED: She glances toward the far edge of the birch stand, where the older trees begin. The light there is already different — heavier, longer horizontals where the birch lets in its vertical white bars. The threshold between them is visible from here.)*

> *(RED: She steps out from the last of the birch trunks and into the edge of the older stand, the light thickening around her into something quieter. The path continues ahead, familiar under her feet, known.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-67-B003] -->

> *(The older trees receive her. The horizontal light comes in long flat planes, quieter than the birch-stand's stripes. The path here is familiar enough that her attention has moved partially inward. At the edge of the path, where the ground goes soft, water sits just under the surface — held there, not visible. An exposed root catches the low horizontal light a few steps ahead, not at the centre of attention.)*

> *(RED: She is walking among the older trees, basket in her right hand, the horizontal light laying long planes across the path. Her attention is elsewhere — somewhere between the weight of the jar and the thought of grandmother's door — when her heel finds the soft edge of the path where water has been sitting just under the surface, unseen.)*

> *(RED: The seam gives. Cold comes through before she has processed the lurch — immediate and specific and wrong, her foot knowing it before the rest of her does. The basket swings hard to the right.)*

> *(RED: She recovers her balance in one quick step, the basket swinging back. She looks down at her right foot. The heel seam has separated along its lower edge. Her sock is already wet.)*

> *(RED: She tips the basket and checks: cloth still in place, the jar's weight sitting where it should, no shift. She presses the bread through the wrapping with two fingers. Still warm.)*

> *(RED: She resets her grip on the basket handle, squares her shoulders slightly, and keeps walking. The wet shoe goes forward with her.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-67-B004] -->

> *(The older trees continue. The path runs through them in near-silence — the birdsong not gone but not close, the ambient sitting just short of quiet. Then the fork is there, earlier than it should be. Two paths, both showing wear, both plausible from here. The cloud cover has shifted; the low sun is not cooperative at this moment, the horizontal light falling across the fork's mouth without giving a clean reading on the roots. The left path's mud shows churning at the edge — foot traffic or animals, the evidence does not settle.)*

> *(RED: She comes around the gentle bend in the older trees and the fork is simply there — present before she expected it, the two paths opening out of the path she's on without announcement.)*

> *(RED: She slows, not quite stopping. Her eyes move between the two paths. Both show wear. Both look like something she could be right about.)*

> *(RED: She looks at the left path's edge where the mud is churned and softened, reading it. The disturbance runs along the margin — irregular, could be boots, could be something with four feet moving through. Neither interpretation closes.)*

> *(RED: Her stride lengthens into the left path before the deliberation has finished. The basket swings across from her right hand to her left in the same motion, weight redistributing, her body already committed while her mind is still mid-sentence. She is on the left path.)*

> *(RED: She keeps walking, the left path receiving her footsteps, the older trees continuing around her.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-67-B005] -->

> *(She is on the left path, the fork behind her, the older trees close on either side. The near-silence of this section of wood — birdsong not absent but not near, the ambient sitting just short of quiet — continues as it has. Then, from behind her, a sound arrives that does not belong to the ambient register. It has weight: not birdsong, not the slow irregular creak of upper branches in slight movement. Something with mass redistributing. A branch settling under sustained pressure. The interval is wrong. It comes from behind her, not from beside or above. It is not loud. It is wrong in its quality.)*

> *(RED: She is walking the left path, the fork already behind her, the older trees pressing close on either side. The ambient sits just short of silence — birdsong somewhere, not near. Her pace is steady. The wet shoe is a background fact.)*

> *(RED: Then a sound arrives from behind her. She registers it before she has categorized it — something with weight, a branch settling not in the way branches settle in wind but under sustained pressure, redistributing. The interval is wrong. It comes from behind her and not from above.)*

> *(RED: She stops.)*

> *(RED: She turns. The path behind her is ordinary in the visible distance — trees, the soft ground, the quality of horizontal light across the roots. Nothing moving. Nothing to resolve the sound into.)*

> *(RED: She waits. The ambient settles back. The sound does not repeat.)*

> *(RED: She turns forward. She tightens her grip on the basket — not checking the contents this time, just holding — and keeps walking. Her pace does not slow. The left path continues ahead of her and she is on it.)*

### SCENE 2 — THE FIRST MEETING

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B001] -->

> *(The left path opens into a section where the canopy stands higher and the trees are set further apart. Light falls in pools of direct sun interrupted by deeper shade. The path's centre-spine is packed firm; a soft depression runs along the right edge where water has collected. An elevated ridge of dry packed earth runs close to the left edge, catching the first pool of direct sun. A lean, dark-coated figure stands near that elevated edge, weight settled, head tilted toward the terrain — already present, occupying the position of a creature at ease in this stretch of wood.)*

> *(RED: She slows her pace by a fraction — not a stop, not a pause anyone would name, just a slight gathering of attention as the canopy opens above her and the path's surface changes under her soles.)*

> *(RED: Her eyes move to the figure near the elevated ridge — a second's clear look, not a stare, before her gaze returns to the path ahead.)*

> *(RED: She shifts the basket slightly in her left hand, confirming the weight of it without looking down.)*

> *(RED: She keeps her line on the firmer centre-spine of the path — not veering away from the figure, not angling toward the soft right edge either — and continues forward at a pace that is steady and purposeful, if not quite what it was.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B002] -->

> *(THE WOLF: It holds its position — weight settled on the dry ridge, head angled toward the packed earth ahead, neither turning toward the sound of her approach nor adjusting its posture to receive it.)*

> *(THE WOLF: A slow shift of its weight from one hind leg to the other — the unhurried ease of a creature adjusting to a comfortable stillness, nothing more. Its gaze remains on the terrain.)*

> *(THE WOLF: It does not turn its head as her footsteps reach the outer edge of easy conversation distance. The listening tilt of its muzzle stays directed at the path's surface — the posture of a creature attending to something already present, already known.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B003] -->

> *(Red has reached the stretch where the Wolf stands near the elevated dry edge. The two figures are now within easy speaking distance on the open path.)*

> *(THE WOLF: Without turning its head toward her, it shifts its gaze along the elevated edge — the dry packed ridge catching the first pool of direct sun — as though taking stock of what the ground is doing.)*

**THE WOLF**  
Dry footing along this left edge, if you don't mind the roots.

> *(THE WOLF: Its attention returns to the terrain ahead — the line delivered into the open air of the path, not held out for a response.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B004] -->

> *(RED: Her pace drops — not a stop, a narrowing of stride — as her attention moves to the elevated ridge on her left and the soft depression on her right.)*

> *(RED: She steps left, the ball of her right foot finding the edge of the elevated ridge first — just the ball, weight still mostly back on the centre-spine, testing what the ground gives.)*

> *(RED: Her weight shifts fully onto the ridge. She adjusts the basket in her left hand to keep it level, then continues forward on the dry packed earth, her stride settling back to its normal rhythm.)*

**RED**  
Yeah, that's — yeah. *(glancing briefly at the ridge underfoot)* Good call.

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B005] -->

> *(THE WOLF: Its gaze moves along the path ahead — the long straight stretch visible from where it stands — as though reading what the ground reports.)*

**THE WOLF**  
Stays firm like this a good while, and once you've come through the straight and the birchwood fork opens on your right — that's the turning for the far houses — the canopy comes back down and the root-crossing wants a little care.

> *(THE WOLF: It settles its weight back, gaze still on the terrain ahead, as though the sentence has already become part of the ground it was describing.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B006] -->

> *(RED: She continues forward on the dry ridge, pace unchanged, receiving the sentence in motion.)*

> *(RED: Her left hand adjusts on the basket handle — a brief, fractional tightening, fingers finding the grip before any instruction reached them. The basket does not move. Her face does not change. Her stride does not break.)*

> *(RED: Her weight keeps going forward. She does not turn her head toward the figure. The ridge is solid under her feet.)*

> *(RED: She walks. The basket stays in her left hand, the grip settled back to ordinary, carrying its warmth forward.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B007] -->

> *(RED: She walks. The elevated ridge is firm under her feet. The next footfall comes, and the next.)*

> *(RED: The basket stays in her left hand. The warmth of the bread is still there through the wrapping.)*

> *(RED: She keeps walking. The light moves between sun and shade across her as the canopy gaps pass above her. Her pace is steady.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B008] -->

> *(THE WOLF: It watches her forward motion from the dry edge — still, weight settled, head at the same terrain-angled tilt it has held throughout.)*

> *(THE WOLF: It remains at the dry edge — still, not following — watching her further down the path until the gaps in the canopy move their light across her back and she carries the basket forward into the long straight stretch.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT issue-68-B009] -->

> *(Red moves further down the left path. The brighter stretch near the dry edge recedes behind her. The long straight section opens ahead, the canopy remaining high and widely spaced, light continuing to fall in alternating pools and shade.)*

> *(RED: She walks the long straight. The high canopy lets the light arrive in open pools. Her feet find the firmer ground without having to look for it now.)*

> *(RED: Her left hand carries the basket. She does not check the contents. The warmth through the cloth is still there — real, ordinary, belonging to the errand.)*

> *(RED: She keeps walking. The dry edge behind her. The straight ahead. Both things carried without ceremony, the way she has been carrying the wet shoe since Scene 1 — present, filed, not closed, going forward.)*
