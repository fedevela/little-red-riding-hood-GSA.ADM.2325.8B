# Little Red Riding Hood

R. Daneel Olivaw

---

# DRAMATIS PERSONAE

**Little Red Riding Hood** — A girl carrying provisions to her grandmother, purposeful, observant, and learning to trust her own judgment.

**The Wolf** — A speaking wolf who knows the woods and presents its pursuit through adaptable offers of help.

---

# SETTING & TIME

**Place:** Woodland paths, including a blocked ordinary road and a rain-marked trace.

**Time:** A timeless folk-tale world; late day after rain.

## ACT 1

### SCENE 1 — THE NECESSARY FORK

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b1] -->

> *(Late-day light filters through the woodland. The path is clear for now; Red carries a basket of provisions, with distant birds and the soft movement of its contents accompanying her steps.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red adjusts the basket securely against her arm, checks that its contents are settled, and continues along the path at a steady pace.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
Grandmother will be glad of these. I’ll arrive with everything whole—and with my own account of the way.

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b2] -->

> *(The path is stopped by a broad, newly fallen beech lying across the road. Its torn branches reach into thorn and steep ground on either side, while the road continues visibly beyond the trunk. The canopy light has cooled, and the remaining daylight is narrowing.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red stops before the beech and shifts the basket to her inner arm. She studies the trunk, the torn branches, and the steep ground on both sides without stepping into either.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: She presses one sturdy shoe against the nearest edge of the road, testing the mud and roots, then looks along the trunk for a passage that will keep the basket clear.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
A fallen tree is not a direction. I’ll find the safest way around it, and I’ll remember where the road continues.

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b3] -->

> *(A calm voice comes from the readable shade beyond the fork, where a lean dark Wolf remains several paces away. It indicates a narrow trace beside the fallen tree. Wet leaves and snapped bracken mark its entrance; farther on, the ground dips out of sight toward low, rain-swollen ground.)*

> *(THE WOLF: The Wolf steps just far enough from the shade for its outline to remain clear, then stops several paces away. It keeps one paw lifted toward the narrow trace beside the fallen tree without entering Red’s way.)*

**THE WOLF**  
That trace will take you around the tree. Keep to the bracken’s edge—the ground dips beyond it, and the wet earth there will sink under your feet.

> *(The narrow trace remains open beside the fallen tree. Its entrance is visible, but the low ground beyond the bracken cannot yet be checked from the fork.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red keeps several paces between herself and the Wolf. She turns slightly so the basket remains protected, then studies the wet leaves and snapped bracken at the trace’s entrance.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
You say the ground sinks beyond the bracken. How far does the trace stay firm, and does it return to the road beyond the low ground?

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: She places a small fallen twig across the road-side edge of the trace as a mark, without entering it, and waits to compare the answer with the ground before choosing.)*

> *(The twig now lies across the trace entrance as Red’s provisional return mark. Beyond it, the bracken parts toward a shallow, water-darkened dip whose far side remains obscured.)*

**THE WOLF**  
It stays firm for about twelve paces, until the bracken thickens. Then it crosses the dip and rises toward the road again. I know it does, though I cannot see the far bank from here; the water may have loosened it since yesterday.

> *(THE WOLF: The Wolf remains several paces back and lowers its paw toward a line of exposed roots along the trace’s near edge.)*

**THE WOLF**  
Use those roots for the first stretch. Test each step after that. I can point out where the path turns, if you want.

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b4] -->

> *(The exposed roots form a possible hand- or foothold along the trace’s near edge. The mud beside them glistens, and the shallow dip remains hidden beyond thickening bracken. The twig mark is visible at the fork.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
You may point out the turn from there. I will test the ground myself.

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red tightens the basket’s cover and keeps one hand free. Without coming closer to the Wolf, she steps onto the first exposed root, presses her weight down, and waits for it to hold.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: She places a second twig upright beside the first, then advances one careful step at a time along the roots, keeping her body turned toward the open road and the Wolf in view.)*

> *(The roots hold beneath Red’s tested weight. Ahead, the trace bends toward thicker bracken and the hidden dip; the open road and the two-twig mark remain behind her.)*

> *(THE WOLF: The Wolf angles its muzzle toward the place where the trace disappears beneath the thickening bracken, remaining several paces away.)*

**THE WOLF**  
There—the path turns left, just before the tallest spray of bracken. Keep your weight on the roots until you reach that bend; the mud opens beside them.

**THE WOLF**  
I can walk along the road’s edge and call the turns from here, or stay where I am and let you find them alone. Choose what leaves you easiest.

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b5] -->

> *(The trace remains passable to the bend, though mud opens beside the roots. The Wolf’s offered choices leave the road edge and the route entrance unobstructed.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
Stay where you are. I have heard the turn.

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red keeps her distance from the Wolf and shifts her weight carefully from root to root. At the bend, she pauses to test the mud with her stick before moving farther.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: She steadies the basket against her hip, glances back to fix the two-twig mark in memory, and faces the narrow trace again.)*

> *(The Wolf remains at the road edge. For a moment the woodland sounds fill the space between them; the trace, the mud, and the two-twig mark remain unchanged.)*

> *(THE WOLF: The Wolf lowers its paw and settles back against the road’s edge, leaving the trace entirely clear. Its head remains turned toward Red, but its listening tilt goes still.)*

**THE WOLF**  
As you wish. The mud beyond the bend is deeper than it looks. Test it before you put your foot down.

> *(THE WOLF: It falls silent, watching the roots and the darkened dip without offering to come nearer.)*

<!-- RESOLVES [BEAT comment-5577156092-next:e1:b6] -->

> *(The last clear light rests on the wet leaves and the trace entrance. The fallen tree still seals the ordinary road, while the narrow route remains open beneath the bracken. Red’s two-twig mark is visible behind her.)*

**LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD**  
I’ll test each step. If the ground gives way, I return to the mark.

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: Red lowers the basket’s cover firmly, plants her stick into the mud beyond the bend, and presses until the ground answers. She steps onto the tested patch, keeping her weight close to the roots.)*

> *(LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: She takes another careful step along the trace, then pauses to look back at the two-twig mark before continuing into the bracken.)*

> *(Red’s steps carry the basket beyond the bend and into the wet-marked trace. The two-twig mark remains behind her, and the Wolf remains at the road edge as the last light thins.)*
